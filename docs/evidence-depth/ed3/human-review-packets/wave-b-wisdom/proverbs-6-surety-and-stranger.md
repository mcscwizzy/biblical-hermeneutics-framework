## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Proverbs 6:1–5 frames the warning with conditional clauses followed by urgent imperatives. Verse 1 uses the surety verb ʿārab, the phrase tāqaʿ kaph (“strike hands”), and the paired terms rēaʿ (“neighbor”) and zār (“stranger”).

**Scholarly interpretation**

Whybray understands ʿārab as guaranteeing a debt to a creditor on another’s behalf and regards hand-striking as a ceremony associated with the pledge; he reads Proverbs 22:27 as showing that the pledged amount is financial. For zār, he reports Boström’s “foreigner” reading and Snijders’s “outsider” proposal, while preferring an Israelite outside one’s family. Whybray says the text does not support a foreign-trade scenario. The record does not establish a specific legal procedure or historical transaction.
