## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Proverbs 28 contains sayings about rulers and public conditions, including contrasts involving a land’s stability, the poor, and oppressive authority; Gilbert treats it as part of a larger 28–29 collection unit.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Gilbert identifies a frame at Proverbs 28:1, 12, 28 and 29:15, 27, and reads the sayings between it as addressing the ruling class. He emphasizes the sages’ social-ethical concern for the poor under corrupt or oppressive power; this reconstruction is reported from the publisher abstract, not treated as the only function of every proverb.
