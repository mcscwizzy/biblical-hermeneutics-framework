## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Ezekiel dates the oracle to the eleventh year, third month, first day (31:1). Verses 2–9 compare Pharaoh and his company to a great cedar sustained by deep waters; verses 10–14 announce its fall, and verses 15–18 portray its descent to Sheol among the trees and nations.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Lee calls the image a “chthonic cedar” and argues that Egyptian associations between trees, Osiris, and the afterlife may help explain the cedar’s connection to Pharaoh’s death and netherworld fate. She also places the image within biblical and ancient Near Eastern royal and cosmic-tree traditions. The Egyptian comparison remains a proposed resonance; the passage does not name Osiris, and the evidence does not establish direct borrowing or a specific ritual setting.
