## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Revelation 13:11–18 describes a second beast that exercises delegated authority, promotes worship of the first beast, makes an image, and links allegiance to buying and selling. The vision does not name a Roman office, priesthood, city, or social class.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Friesen accepts a general relationship between the land beast and Roman imperial cults, then argues from inscriptions and the social setting of Roman Asia that the image evokes elite families operating through a network of provincial and local cult offices, municipal representatives, priesthoods, and public events. He reviews narrower proposals such as a single priesthood or provincial council but argues that the symbol is not a one-to-one cipher. The inscriptions document institutions and elite participation; they do not by themselves prove that every element of the vision maps directly to a specific historical actor or that worship was uniformly enforced across the empire.
