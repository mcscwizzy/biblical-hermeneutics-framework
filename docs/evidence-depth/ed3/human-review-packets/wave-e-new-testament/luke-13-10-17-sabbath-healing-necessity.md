## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The synagogue leader says that people must work during six days and tells the crowd to seek healing then; Jesus responds that this daughter of Abraham, bound for eighteen years, ought to be released on the Sabbath. The dialogue repeats the root δεῖ, while the following animal-care example invokes a familiar weekly practice.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Kilgallen argues that the leader’s statement and Jesus’ response both turn on necessity: the leader accepts healing but objects to its Sabbath timing, while Jesus frames the woman’s release as something that ought to occur on that day. Kilgallen reads the routine leading of an ox or donkey to water as a lesser-to-greater argument, distinct from an emergency rescue. This is an interpretation of Luke’s rhetoric and does not establish a uniform rule across Jewish communities.
