## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The community speaks of rejection, a shaken land, defeat, and need for rescue (vv. 3–6, 10–13 in common English numbering). The heading associates the psalm with David and Edom, but the body names no campaign.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Verde argues the general defeat language and Edom references suit post-exilic use, and that Book II’s placement links the psalm to the memory of Jerusalem’s fall. He treats the superscription as theological recontextualization; composition date and first event remain disputed.
