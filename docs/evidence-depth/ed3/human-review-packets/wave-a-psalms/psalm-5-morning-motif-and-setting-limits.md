## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

In Hebrew numbering, the speaker says that in the morning he lays his request before YHWH and waits (v. 4; English/ASV v. 3); the poem then speaks of entry to YHWH’s house (Hebrew v. 8; English v. 7) and contrasts evildoers with those who take refuge (Hebrew vv. 5–7, 10–13; English vv. 4–6, 9–12). No named sanctuary or date appears.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha reviews readings of Psalm 5 as prayer for help in a legal dispute accompanied by a morning sacrifice, but cautions that the terse wording does not establish a precise time or temple location. He proposes that the psalm’s present placement among Psalms 3–7 frames it as David’s prayer during flight from Absalom and addresses later community conflict; that is a redactional interpretation, not a recoverable original event.
