## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The speaker's repeated confession of refuge gives way to plural commands to trust and pour out the heart before God, and then to warnings about illicit gain and wealth.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha argues that the communal address belongs to the poem's unity rather than signaling a separate insertion: the speaker's example supports instruction against reliance on human power or wealth. He proposes a post-exilic setting shaped by exploitation and competition in Jerusalem; that socioeconomic scenario is one scholarly reconstruction, not an event independently established by the poem.
