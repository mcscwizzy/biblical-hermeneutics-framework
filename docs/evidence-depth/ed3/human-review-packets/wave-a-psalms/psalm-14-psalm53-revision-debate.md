## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Psalm 14 and Psalm 53 share most of their sequence and wording but differ at several points, including how the wicked are described and the formulation of their terror and fate. Their divine-name usage also reflects the Psalter’s Elohistic collection.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha argues that Psalm 53 is an intentional adaptation of Psalm 14, revised for its current place among Psalms 52–55 and its wisdom-oriented reading of David’s story. He acknowledges the reverse proposal—that Psalm 14 developed from Psalm 53—but rejects it as a less adequate explanation of the differences. The text establishes close literary reuse; it does not by itself establish when, by whom, or in which direction the poems were first composed. No cross-passage relationship edge is authored here.
