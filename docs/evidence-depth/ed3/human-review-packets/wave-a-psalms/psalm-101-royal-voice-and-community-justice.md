## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The speaker opens by singing of steadfast love and justice, vows to walk with integrity, rejects slander and deceit, and closes with the repeated morning judgment of evildoers in the city.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha analyzes the poem as a tightly arranged royal prayer with a personal sphere in verses 1–4 and a public sphere in verses 5–8. He proposes that wisdom and Torah piety shape its use within Book IV as a critique of corrupt Judean leadership and a plea for Yahweh’s just rule. The article proposes a late Persian or early Hellenistic horizon, while surveying live disputes about genre, unity, segmentation, and date; no particular king, enthronement ceremony, or event is established.
