## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Psalm 57 moves from an urgent plea against persecutors to confidence, praise among the peoples, and a closing appeal; the latter sequence is reused in Psalm 108 with wording and placement changes.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha argues that the composer of Psalm 108 replaced Psalm 60’s lament with praise drawn from Psalm 57. In Psalm 57 the reused lines follow an individual plea and function as confidence that motivates praise; in Psalm 108 their new opening position gives them the quality of an individual praise song. Botha treats this as literary recontextualization, not evidence for a particular battle or a recoverable biography of David.
