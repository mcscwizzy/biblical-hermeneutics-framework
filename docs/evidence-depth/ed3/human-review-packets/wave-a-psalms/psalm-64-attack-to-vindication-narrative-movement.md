## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The speaker asks for protection from plotted attacks; the enemies sharpen their tongues like swords and aim cruel words like arrows. God then shoots an arrow, the attackers stumble over their own tongues, observers respond in fear and understanding, and the righteous take refuge and rejoice.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Eder proposes two main movements: the petitioner and enemies are characterized in the first part, while divine intervention and its effects structure the second. She highlights the poem's forward-driving parallelism and growing tension. Eder also states that narratology has limits and must be supplemented by semantic and metaphor analysis; the narrative reading does not prove a specific historical event.
