## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Papyrus Amherst 63, col. XII, lines 11–19, is an Aramaic prayer written in Demotic script. Its translated petitions to be answered, helped, and granted requests correspond in sequence and vocabulary to petitions in Psalm 20:1–5 (Hebrew numbering 20:2–6). In Ward’s proposed reading, the prayer invokes Aḥor, Bethel, and Ba’al Shamayin and places Aḥor at Zaphon.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Ward’s full transcription and commentary documents the parallel but also emphasizes uncertainty in reading Demotic signs as Aramaic and in identifying a divine name. He surveys different models: Nims and Steiner treat the papyrus as a paganized adaptation of Psalm 20, while other scholarship proposes that the biblical psalm reoriented an earlier northern/Samarian prayer. Kister’s article abstract confirms recent comparative study but does not expose its detailed conclusions. The available evidence establishes a literary parallel, not its direction, a direct copying event, or Psalm 20’s date.
