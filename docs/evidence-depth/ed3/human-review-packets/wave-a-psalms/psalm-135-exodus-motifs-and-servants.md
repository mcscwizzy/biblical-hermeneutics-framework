## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Psalm 135:1 addresses YHWH’s servants in the temple; verses 8–9 recall the striking of Egypt’s firstborn and signs against Pharaoh and his servants. The poem places these historical deeds after a creation section and before the indictment of powerless idols.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Human reads the human-and-animal firstborn in verse 8 as a merism emphasizing the scope of the plague tradition and notes its relation to Exodus 12:12, 29. He also reads “servants” in verses 1 and 9 contrastively: YHWH’s servants are summoned to praise, while Pharaoh’s servants experience the narrated judgment. This is the psalm’s theological use of Exodus memory, not independent evidence for the events or a specific festival performance.
