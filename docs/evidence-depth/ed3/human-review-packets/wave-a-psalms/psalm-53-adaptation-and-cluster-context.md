## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Psalm 53 follows a broad sequence shared with Psalm 14 but has distinct wording and a different closing depiction of the enemies’ fate. Botha analyzes it as a six-line poem in Hebrew verse numbering, with recurring words and inclusio-like framing.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha argues that Psalm 53 is an intentionally adapted form of Psalm 14, revised to function in the Psalms 52–55 cluster. He connects the cluster’s superscriptions with the David–Saul narrative as a final-form reading, while acknowledging the reverse proposal that Psalm 14 developed from Psalm 53. Neither the cluster’s headings nor the proposed literary setting establishes Davidic authorship or the historical occasion of composition.
