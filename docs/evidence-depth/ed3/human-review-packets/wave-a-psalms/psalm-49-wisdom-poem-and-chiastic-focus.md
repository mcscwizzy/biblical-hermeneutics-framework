## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The poem addresses all peoples, announces a wisdom saying, warns against trusting wealth, and contrasts mortal human status with God’s power to redeem from Sheol.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Pleins argues in the abstract that Psalm 49’s chiastic form focuses its wisdom on wealth, death, and endurance: accumulated wealth cannot prevent death, while divine action—not human wealth—provides protection. Since the full article was inaccessible, the precise strophic map and disputed language about the grave are not reconstructed here, and no settled account of Israelite afterlife belief is inferred.

The poem’s difficult statements about Sheol and redemption require fuller text-critical and scholarly review.
