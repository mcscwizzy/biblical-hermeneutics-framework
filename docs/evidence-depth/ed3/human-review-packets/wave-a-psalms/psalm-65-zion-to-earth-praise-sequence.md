## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The poem names Zion, God's courts and temple, the earth's farthest reaches, seas, waters, grain, rain, pastures, hills, meadows, and valleys; its closing creation imagery joins agricultural abundance with praise.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha reads Psalm 65 as the opening of an editorially arranged Psalms 65–68 cluster. In his account, sanctuary and Zion frame a wider presentation of God's rule and care for creation, and the rain and harvest of Psalm 65 contribute to a universal horizon. He argues that erets in the rain passage is better rendered ‘earth’ than ‘land’ in this cluster context. The proposed literary arrangement and translation judgment do not establish a specific harvest festival, agricultural rite, or historical event.
