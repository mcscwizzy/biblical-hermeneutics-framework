## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The superscription associates the shiggaion with David and “the words of Cush, a Benjaminite.” The speaker asks for rescue from pursuers and describes life-threatening danger, but the poem itself names no encounter in Samuel.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Berger argues that the superscription invites comparison with a wider set of Samuel narratives in which David restrains himself toward Saul and other Benjaminites; the abstract places the Cush episode during David’s flight from Saul and relates the poem to 1 Samuel 24 and 1 Chronicles 12. Obiorah reports that some ancient Greek and other versions read “Cushite,” prompting a proposed link to the messenger announcing Absalom’s death in 2 Samuel 18; she judges the poem’s threat language a poor fit for a dirge. These are competing reconstructions, not independently verified biographies. Berger’s full article was not accessible.
