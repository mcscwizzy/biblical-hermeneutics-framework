## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The enemies return in the evening, growl around the city, and may remain through the night; the speaker answers with a vow to sing of God's strength and goodness in the morning and calls God a fortress and refuge.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Janowski reads the poem as two corresponding movements from petition and complaint to trust and praise, with repeated evening lines and a repeated trust refrain. He notes that scholars disagree over whether the second evening unit is a refrain or a textual variant, and that the repeated description intensifies the enemies' threat. The time pattern is a literary feature, not proof of a particular historical siege.
