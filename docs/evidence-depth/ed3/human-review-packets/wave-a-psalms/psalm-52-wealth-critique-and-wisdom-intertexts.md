## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

The poem indicts a mighty figure for boasting in evil, using a destructive tongue, and trusting abundant wealth. It then contrasts that figure's uprooting with the speaker's trust in God's steadfast love and the green-olive-tree image.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Botha argues that the rich antagonist and trusting speaker apply wisdom teaching to social exploitation, and that Psalm 52 evokes Proverbs 1–3 and Proverbs 11:28 as well as Psalm 49 and Jeremiah 9. He proposes a late Persian-period community context. Botha notes that Hossfeld and Zenger regard the connections as less direct and consider an earlier form possible; the proposed allusions, their direction, and the date are not settled findings.
