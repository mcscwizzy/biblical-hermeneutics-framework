## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

Psalm 108’s oracle assigns territories and describes Ephraim as the speaker’s helmet and Judah as a ruler’s staff; the poem closes with confidence that help and victory come from God.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Sutton and Human read head imagery as warfare language that communicates honour and shame, and interpret Psalm 108 as the opening movement in a literary sequence with Psalms 109–110: petition for rescue, assault and accusation, then victory. The source’s verse numbers count the heading, so its references to Psalm 108:9 and 14 correspond to BHF body verses 8 and 13. The sequence and proposed post-exilic royal horizon are interpretations, not proof of an actual campaign or composition date.
