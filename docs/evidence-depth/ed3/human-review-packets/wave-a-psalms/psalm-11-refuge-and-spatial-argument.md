## Claim under review

**Primary textual observation**

One voice asks why the speaker should flee like a bird; the speaker instead confesses refuge in YHWH. The poem locates YHWH in the holy temple and on a heavenly throne, then closes with the upright seeing YHWH’s face.

**Scholarly interpretation**

Prinsloo reads the poem through movement between “off-centre” danger and “at-centre” refuge: the hunted righteous are threatened, while YHWH’s temple and heavenly throne provide the poem’s center of judgment and presence. He interprets the final restoration language as a spatial answer to suffering and social dislocation. This is a literary account of the poem’s rhetoric, not a description of a recoverable worship rite.
