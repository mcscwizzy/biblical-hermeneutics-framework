## Form, vocabulary, and movement

The psalm opens with a request that God be gracious, bless “us,” and make the divine face shine (v. 1). The purpose clause turns outward: God’s way and salvation are to be known among the nations (v. 2). A central wish calls peoples to rejoice because God judges and guides them (vv. 3–4). A second petition for blessing encloses the poem (vv. 6–7). Verse 6 mentions the earth’s produce; it does not name a harvest festival.

Botha counts eleven of the poem’s fourteen verbs as jussives and emphasizes the repeated wish/petition. The Hebrew forms and translations are debated in prior interpretations; the item states the requests in the text and attributes the form-critical count and rhetorical proposal to Botha.

## Setting and scholarly disagreement

Botha challenges the long-standing harvest-thanksgiving reading and argues that the poem calls for God to display honor publicly so that peoples recognize Israel’s relationship with God. Other proposals include thanksgiving for harvest and prayer for rain. The single produce reference does not decide the occasion. The poem’s first-person plural speaker plausibly represents a worshipping community, with the nations also imagined as hearers; a specific season, rite, or date is not identified.
