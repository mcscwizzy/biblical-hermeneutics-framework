# ED-2A source bibliography and verification

All listed source records are independently attributed. Locators are passage-specific where the publication permits. Quotations in the CKL are short descriptions in the agent’s own words; no proprietary commentary or lexicon text is reproduced. The source and locator fields added to the Psalms object expose the publications used for each evidence item.

## Additional primary texts and reception sources used in the packets

These are cited in the research packets as primary texts or later reception, not conflated with the original literary setting. They are not separate ED-2A evidence items.

- Masoretic Psalter, Psalm passages at the exact anchors in the evidence records (`psalms-masoretic`).
- 1 Chronicles 16:23–33, canonical reuse of material parallel to Psalm 96; it does not establish the psalm’s original direction or date.
- Greek Psalm 95 superscription (MT Psalm 96), in Rahlfs–Hanhart, framing the psalm with a rebuilding-after-captivity setting.
- Matthew 5:5 and Greek Psalm 36:11 (MT Psalm 37:11), later Christian reuse/translation reception.
- Romans 8:36, explicit later Christian citation of Psalm 44:23.
- Midrash Tehillim 30:6, later Jewish identifications of Psalm 30 with multiple Temple dedications.
- Babylonian Talmud, [*Makkot* 24a](https://www.sefaria.org/Makkot.24a?lang=bi), later Jewish reading of Psalm 15’s ethical catalogue as David’s summary of commandments.
- Babylonian Talmud, [*Berakhot* 4b](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.4b?lang=bi), later Jewish discussion of the missing nun in Psalm 145 and the daily recitation of the psalm.
- Nino Brozinčević, “The Seven Penitential Psalms: Historical Origin and Determination of Genre,” *Obnovljeni Život* 79, no. 4 (2024): 489–500, [DOI 10.31337/oz.79.4.7](https://doi.org/10.31337/oz.79.4.7). Used to distinguish the late-antique/medieval Christian reception category from Psalm 6’s original genre.
- For Psalm 37: W. Domeris, “Meek or Oppressed? Reading Matthew 5:5 in Context,” *Acta Theologica Supplementum* 23 (2016): 131–149, [DOI 10.4314/actat.v23i1S.7](https://doi.org/10.4314/actat.v23i1S.7); [article PDF](https://www.wisdomlib.org/uploads/journals/actat/2016_supplementum-23_146043_135556.pdf). Used for later translation/reception, not to define Psalm 37’s original audience.
- For Psalm 47’s later Jewish liturgical reception: *Arukh ha-Shulḥan*, *Orach Chayim* 585:4, [text and translation](https://voices.sefaria.org/sheets/338730), custom of reciting Psalm 47 seven times before shofar blasts; the source is later than the psalm and does not establish an original Rosh Hashanah setting. A modern discussion calls the custom’s widespread form relatively recent ([Torah.org](https://torah.org/torah-portion/mikra-5773-netzavim/)).

## Identity and integrity rules applied

All twelve new CKL source IDs were checked against source IDs across the CKL object tree before insertion. Each refers to one publication or catalog record and has its own title, author/organization, publisher, year, locator, URL, source type, and `supports` list. Existing IDs were reused only when they already identify the relevant base text (`psalms-masoretic`) or broad existing CKL material; a broad source record was not substituted for a pinpoint source edition.
