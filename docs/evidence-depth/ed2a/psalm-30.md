## Poetic movement and translation

The superscription identifies a “song for the dedication of the house” and associates it with David. The poem then recalls a plea from a crisis, thanksgiving for rescue, and a reversal from mourning to joy. Its shifts between praise, plea, and thanksgiving make a single performance scenario difficult to establish.

The Hebrew `le-David` can be heard as a Davidic association, and the syntax around “dedication of the house” allows different construals, including a dedication associated with David’s house. English versions that supply “Temple” make an interpretive identification the Hebrew title itself does not name. Sommer argues that terse language and verbal forms let the poem serve different worshippers and circumstances.

## Setting and audience

The title is evidence of the psalm’s received framing as a dedication song, not independent proof of the original occasion. The poem does not name a specific building, king, dedication ceremony, or date. A worshipping audience reusing a prayer of crisis and thanksgiving is plausible, but not uniquely recoverable.

## Ancient sources and reception

- **Primary source:** Masoretic Psalm 30:1–12.
- **Later Jewish reception:** Midrash Tehillim 30:6 connects the psalm with Solomon’s dedication, the Second Temple, and the Maccabean rededication. These are successive reception settings and should not be collapsed into the psalm’s original occasion.
- **Later Christian reception:** no passage-specific Christian reinterpretation was added to the item; a dedication theme alone does not establish a historical Christian origin or use.
