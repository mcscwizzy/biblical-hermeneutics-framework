# Recommended next Psalms research wave

The current audit still identifies 66 Psalms chapters with no eligible CKL material after ED-2A. The following candidates span different forms and offer passage-specific questions visible in the texts. This is a research queue, not a claim that the proposed questions have already been source-verified; each should first pass the ED-1B source and evidence standard and an existing-record reuse check.

| Candidate | Text-led research need to verify | Why it is a useful next candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psalm 55 | Individual lament with betrayal, city violence, and a shifting speaker’s address | Extends the lament work beyond the body-language and communal forms in this wave. |
| Psalm 60 | Communal defeat, military imagery, and a superscription naming a historical setting | Tests how to handle historical headings without treating them as independent proof of an event. |
| Psalm 66 | Praise, thanksgiving, and an invitation to hear a first-person testimony | Adds a thanksgiving form and a potential individual-to-community movement. |
| Psalm 75 | Thanksgiving framed by divine judgment and the “cup” image | Adds royal/judicial imagery with a focused translation and metaphor question. |
| Psalm 80 | Repeated restoration refrain and vine imagery in a communal appeal | Adds another communal lament and an opportunity to distinguish historical proposals from the poem’s refrain. |
| Psalm 81 | Festival summons, musical instructions, and a divine speech within the poem | Offers a strong worship-context question while requiring care around proposed festival identification. |
| Psalm 97 | Kingship hymn with cloud, fire, and divine attendants | Useful for testing when divine-council language is warranted by the passage and when it is not. |
| Psalm 100 | Thanksgiving entrance summons addressed to all the earth | Extends the worship-entry question in Psalm 15 with a different genre and explicit audience. |

Selection should be refreshed from the latest census before work begins. Research priority should depend on the availability of passage-specific primary and scholarly sources, not on thematic familiarity or the desire to eliminate every zero-coverage chapter.
