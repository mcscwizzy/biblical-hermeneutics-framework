# Psalm 72 research packet

## Research gap and decision

ED-1A found no `evidence_items[]` directly scoped to Psalm 72. The existing reader-visible claims address the psalm’s placement at the end of Book II (72:18–20) and the superscription tradition (72:1). The useful gap is a source-grounded account of the royal prayer’s justice language and its Ancient Near Eastern kingship setting, with the limits of historical reconstruction made clear.

**Decision:** accepted for one new item, `psalm-72-royal-justice-setting`, anchored exactly to Psalm 72:1–14.

## Primary observation

The poem asks God to give the king justice and righteousness, expects him to defend the afflicted and needy and oppose the oppressor, and depicts rule reaching other peoples and kings (Ps 72:1–4, 8–14). The source is the existing CKL primary-text record `psalms-masoretic`, whose locator is Psalms 1–150 in the Masoretic textual tradition.

## Scholarly interpretation and setting

D. J. Human classifies Psalm 72 among the royal psalms and reads Israelite kingship within an Ancient Near Eastern setting where kings bore political and religious responsibilities. He notes distinctive Israelite features and reads the psalm as a prayer for the king. Human proposes that its court style may reflect composition by a court poet or cultic prophet for an official royal event, but labels this probable; he identifies the historical and cultic setting, historical versus ideal king, and date as debated. The item therefore records the royal setting as a scholarly proposal, not as an identified original occasion.

The psalm’s original audience is not named. Its royal address and prayer form support a royal/cultic setting as a possibility, but the item does not infer a specific court ceremony or reign. No absolute composition date is asserted.

## Acceptance and limitations

The item passed the pilot checks: exact chapter-scoped anchor; text observation separate from scholarship; original-setting proposal marked as provisional; no historical king or absolute date asserted; no duplicate of the anthology or superscription claims. Confidence is medium and the record marks historical uncertainty.

Deferred: adjudicating among proposed historical kings and composition dates would require a broader review of competing Psalm 72 scholarship. This pilot does not resolve that debate.
