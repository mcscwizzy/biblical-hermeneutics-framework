# 2 Chronicles 30 research packet

## Research gap and decision

ED-1A found no `evidence_items[]` directly scoped to 2 Chronicles 30. The existing claims already cover Hezekiah’s Passover across chapters 29–31 and the book’s Judah-centered literary pattern. The useful gap is a bounded treatment of the invitation to northern Israelites and its later community significance, without treating the narrative as independently verified event history.

**Decision:** accepted for one new item, `second-chronicles-30-northern-invitation`, anchored exactly to 2 Chronicles 30:6–12.

## Primary observation

The chapter reports couriers taking Hezekiah’s invitation throughout Israel and Judah. Some recipients mock the couriers; people from Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun humble themselves and come; Judah is described as united in response (2 Chr 30:6–12). The primary-text record is the existing CKL source `2-chronicles-1-36`, whose locator is 2 Chronicles 1:1–36:23.

## Scholarly interpretation and setting

Gary N. Knoppers treats the invitation as a case study in how postmonarchic Judean writers represented relations with northern Israel. He reads Hezekiah’s message as affirming the continuing claims of the Davidic line and Jerusalem sanctuary for a broad Israel. His abstract places the studied Judean texts in a late Achaemenid or early Hellenistic composition context. That is a composition-era interpretation, separate from the story’s setting in Hezekiah’s reign in the late eighth century BCE.

The invitation and response are claims made by Chronicles; Knoppers’s study does not independently attest that the narrated Passover occurred exactly as described. No claim about the historical event is added from Sennacherib’s campaign.

## Acceptance and limitations

The item passed the pilot checks: exact chapter-scoped anchor; primary narrative observation separate from Knoppers’s historical-literary reading; Hezekiah’s narrative horizon distinguished from the proposed composition horizon; no independent corroboration or archaeological inference claimed. Confidence is medium and the record marks chronological uncertainty.

Deferred: independent evidence for the particular Passover as narrated, and further comparison of competing dates for Chronicles, require research outside this bounded item.
