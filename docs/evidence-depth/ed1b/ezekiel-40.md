# Ezekiel 40 research packet

## Research gap and decision

ED-1A found no `evidence_items[]` directly scoped to Ezekiel 40. Existing claims address the historical horizon, practical outline, and temple vision across the larger book or vision block. The useful gap is the chapter’s relative date and the opening measured tour as a literary vision report, while keeping that report distinct from archaeological evidence of a built structure.

**Decision:** accepted for one new item, `ezekiel-40-vision-measuring-sequence`, anchored exactly to Ezekiel 40:1–16.

## Primary observation

Ezekiel dates the vision to the twenty-fifth year of exile and fourteenth year after Jerusalem’s fall (40:1). He is brought to the land in a vision, told to look, listen, and report to Israel, and shown a bronze-like man who measures the wall and east gateway (40:2–16). The primary-text record is the existing CKL source `ezekiel-masoretic`, locator Ezekiel 1–48 in the Masoretic textual tradition.

## Scholarly interpretation and setting

Michael A. Lyons identifies explicit vision language in Ezekiel 40:2, seeing/showing terminology, and the continuing guide as formal features of the literary vision report in Ezekiel 40–48. He considers the bulk of the material exilic and Ezekiel 40–42 best situated before a temple existed (before 515 BCE), but allows Persian-period additions and says more specific dating is uncertain. The item treats that as Lyons’s qualified composition proposal, not a settled date for the final chapter.

The chapter’s narrated audience is the house of Israel, to whom Ezekiel is told to report what he sees (40:4). Its measuring sequence belongs to the vision narrative; it does not itself establish that the measured temple was built or surveyed as described.

## Acceptance and limitations

The item passed the pilot checks: exact chapter-scoped anchor; relative narrated date separated from composition date; vision-report interpretation attributed to Lyons; date and compositional layers qualified; no speculative site identification, construction claim, or archaeology catalog record added. Confidence is medium and the record marks interpretive uncertainty.

Deferred: the redaction history and physical interpretation of the larger temple vision remain disputed and require a broader scholarly review. ED-1B does not turn this measured vision into a real-world building plan.
