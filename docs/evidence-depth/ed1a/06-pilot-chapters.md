# ED-1A ten pilot chapters

The pilots cover Torah, historical narrative, poetry/wisdom, prophets, a Gospel, and an epistle. The set deliberately mixes lean chapters with already well-covered controls. “Eligible items” and “source-traceable placements” are current projection counts; they are not quality scores. Exact per-chapter detail is in `chapter-coverage.csv`.

## 1. Genesis 14 — Torah, comparative history and literary setting

- **Current evidence:** 15 eligible CKL items; 13 source-traceable child placements. Examples include `ckl:genesis-abraham-call-covenant-context#evidence:g14-campaign-geography-itinerary`, `#evidence:g14-king-identifications-disputed`, `#evidence:g14-melchizedek-king-priest-salem`, `#evidence:g14-melchizedek-reception-boundary`, and `#evidence:g14-siddim-bitumen-pits`.
- **Important context/question:** What can be established about the coalition campaign, names, route, bitumen pits, and Melchizedek from the text and its sources? Distinguish literary/historical questions from efforts to assign patriarchal dates or identify every king.
- **Underused evidence:** The chapter has a clear “disputed identifications” record, which can model how uncertainty is presented. No source-traceable ancient-primary-source evidence is currently typed for Genesis 14.
- **Sources:** Primary text: Genesis 14. A primary comparative source to evaluate is the much later Bisitun inscription, as discussed by `g4a-naaman-gen14-kings`; it is not confirmation of Genesis 14's events. Secondary sources: `g4a-van-seters-abraham`, `g4a-granerod-melchizedek`, `g4a-nissenbaum-siddim-bitumen`, `g4a-local-sarna-genesis`, and `g4a-local-hamilton-genesis-1-17`.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Review the claim/evidence boundary and add only a concise, separately sourced evidence item if it can improve on the existing cluster—especially chronology and what each comparison does not establish. Readers should see how historical comparison works without being told that a parallel proves dependence.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** High uncertainty on names, chronology, and historical identification; high curation complexity.

## 2. Exodus 12 — Torah, Passover and Egyptian royal context

- **Current evidence:** 24 eligible items; 9 source-traceable child placements; 3 items are not assigned to a section. Current records include `ckl:egyptian-kingship-and-divine-order#evidence:judgment-on-egypts-gods` and `#evidence:seti-royal-offering-scene`, with a caution note on Exodus 12:12.
- **Important context/question:** How do the text's household ritual, calendar, sacrifice, memory, and anti-royal language work together? What historical models are proposed for the event and composition, and which cannot be tested by the available material?
- **Underused evidence:** The existing Seti I offering-table artifact gives Egyptian royal-cultic context, not evidence that the specific plague narrative happened. The current projection has no source-traceable ancient-primary-source typed item for this chapter.
- **Sources:** Primary text: Exodus 12. The Metropolitan Museum's [Offering Table of Seti I](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/549218) is a primary material artifact from the New Kingdom, Dynasty 19, not evidence of the Exodus narrative. `museum-bible-merneptah-stele` is a separate Egyptian inscription source. Secondary sources: `sarna-exodus-1991`, `meyers-exodus-2005`, `exodus-royal-conflict-text`, `numbers-33-judgment-text`, and `zondervan-illustrated-pentateuch-backgrounds`. Passage relevance for each artifact or inscription must be explained and qualified.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Pilot a layered presentation that separates the biblical text, Egyptian setting, later Passover practice, and Christian reception. Do not present an artifact as corroboration of the Exodus or flatten later Jewish practice into a direct report of the text's first audience.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** High historical and compositional uncertainty; high curation complexity.

## 3. 2 Kings 18 — Historical narrative, Assyrian sources and archaeology

- **Current evidence:** 34 eligible items; 21 source-traceable placements. Exact current records include `ckl:sennacherib-prism#evidence:taylor-prism-annals`, `#evidence:lachish-relief-royal-siege-rhetoric`, `ckl:assyrian-tribute-and-royal-representation#evidence:sennacherib-hezekiah-tribute`, and a source-attributed caution note `ckl:assyrian-tribute-and-royal-representation#interpretive-note:sha256-957c8a83f2d31504af685ea49431f31342d3b63105a1dce61520d1d3f93777a9`.
- **Important context/question:** What do Kings, Isaiah, Sennacherib's royal annals, and the Lachish reliefs each claim, and where do their scopes and emphases differ?
- **Underused evidence:** Strong evidence is already present. The opportunity is its reusable display as calibrated primary-source comparison, with date, genre, royal purpose, and disagreement visible—not another cluster of paraphrases.
- **Sources:** Primary sources: Sennacherib's annals in `taylor-prism-bm-91032` / `sennacherib-rinap3-1` and the Lachish palace reliefs (`lachish-relief-bm-124911`). The British Museum [Taylor Prism record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1855-1003-1) describes the tribute account; its [Lachish relief record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1856-0909-14_5) catalogs a particular panel and find context. Secondary sources: `ussishkin-lachish-1982`, `cogan-tadmor-second-kings`, and `younger-conquest-accounts`.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Use this as a control pilot to test provenance and reader presentation of already-accepted evidence. Preserve the distinction between Hezekiah's tribute, Assyrian siege rhetoric, the biblical narrative's account of Jerusalem's outcome, and excavated Lachish remains.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** Moderate to high; moderate curation complexity because the cluster is already mature.

## 4. 2 Chronicles 30 — Historical narrative, historiography and festival

- **Current evidence:** 9 eligible items; 3 source-traceable child placements. Examples: `ckl:2-chronicles#claim:second-chronicles-hezekiah-passover` and the contextual note `ckl:assyrian-deportation-and-provincial-incorporation#interpretive-note:sha256-c94867378f4e48abb5631b3ad20e21842d88989b448210679374dfa58b61779a`.
- **Important context/question:** How does the Chronicler narrate northern participation and the delayed Passover in the literary context of Hezekiah's reforms? How should readers distinguish this presentation from Kings and from modern reconstructions of the eighth-century event?
- **Underused evidence:** The current claim spans 2 Chronicles 29–30, while the chapter-specific record set is small. Existing Assyrian/Samaria context is available, but does not independently document the festival.
- **Sources:** Primary text: 2 Chronicles 30, with Kings as a separate biblical witness and Sennacherib's annals as imperial-period context only. No contemporary external record of this particular Passover is established by the current audit. Secondary sources: `2-chronicles-1-36`, `klein-second-chronicles`, `radner-israel-house-omri`, and `cogan-binding-samaria-2022`.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Research one narrowly scoped historical-literary record on the narrative's chronology and treatment of northern Israel, with separate support for the biblical account and its imperial setting. Explain where reconstruction remains uncertain.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** High; high curation complexity.

## 5. Psalm 72 — Poetry, royal ideology and reception

- **Current evidence:** 4 eligible items and 2 traceable child placements. The current set includes `ckl:psalms#claim:psalms-five-book-anthology` (which anchors Psalm 72:18–20 among its references) and `ckl:psalms#claim:psalms-superscription-collections`.
- **Important context/question:** How do the poem's ideal ruler, justice for the poor, international horizon, and Solomon superscription function in the psalm? What evidence supports alternative dates or royal settings? What is later reception, and what remains the poem's own imagery?
- **Underused evidence:** The current eligible records primarily frame the Psalter's collection and superscriptions; the audit found no dedicated source-traceable historical/cultural or primary-source record for the psalm.
- **Sources:** Primary text: Psalm 72 in `psalms-masoretic`. Secondary sources: `declaisse-walford-jacobson-tanner`, `oxford-psalter-metanarrative`, `goldingay-psalms`, and `baker-introducing-psalms`. Consider other ancient royal texts only when a scholar establishes a useful comparison; do not infer direct dependence from shared royal language.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Add a passage-focused literary/historical study only after comparing major readings and their evidence. This tests how the standard treats date, superscription, genre, and reception without requiring archaeology.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** Moderate to high; moderate curation complexity.

## 6. Proverbs 31 — Wisdom poetry, acrostic and household economy

- **Current evidence:** 6 eligible items; 5 source-traceable placements. The directly relevant record is `ckl:proverbs#claim:proverbs-capable-woman-acrostic`, anchored to Proverbs 31:10–31. `ckl:proverbs#claim:proverbs-greek-order` also appears in the chapter's unclassified list, but its own references concern Proverbs 24 and 30; it is not evidence about Proverbs 31 without further review.
- **Important context/question:** How do acrostic form, household labor, textile production, trade, and wisdom instruction shape the poem? Is it an individual portrait, an idealized literary figure, or a concluding pedagogical voice? How do later readings differ?
- **Underused evidence:** Literary form is present; the chapter has no source-traceable historical/cultural or archaeological/geography record. The latter absence is not automatically a defect.
- **Sources:** Primary text: Proverbs 31 in `proverbs-masoretic`. Secondary sources: `fox-proverbs-ten-thirty-one`, `cambridge-proverbs-thirty-one` (Jacqueline Vayntrub, 2020), and `dell-theology-proverbs`. No ancient comparative source is required unless it bears on a specific textual question.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Review a compact source-backed record on the poem's form and economic imagery, preserving the distinction between an idealized literary depiction and empirical claims about all ancient women. Add a material-culture claim only if an artifact directly helps readers understand a named practice.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** Moderate; moderate curation complexity.

## 7. Ezekiel 40 — Prophetic literature, visionary space and temple roles

- **Current evidence:** 12 eligible items; 3 source-traceable child placements. Current records include `ckl:ezekiel#claim:ezekiel-temple-vision` (Ezekiel 40:1–49 and 43:1–27), `#claim:ezekiel-historical-horizon`, and `#claim:ezekiel-practical-outline`. `ckl:new-jerusalem-prophecy` appears unclassified in this view.
- **Important context/question:** How does the exilic setting and vision genre shape the detailed measurements, priestly offices, and spatial sequence? Which readings understand the vision as ideal, symbolic, future, or otherwise, and what are their textual arguments?
- **Underused evidence:** The current direct cluster has limited source-traceable child placements and no archaeology/geography tag. Primary text: Ezekiel 40–48, read alongside relevant biblical temple texts; any later Second Temple source belongs in reception history. Secondary sources include `block-ezekiel-25-48`, `ganzel-priests-prince`, and `sulzbach-future-map`; they can support literary, priestly, and spatial discussion without implying a built plan.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Build an evidence map that explains internal spatial relationships, historical horizon, and interpretive alternatives. Keep later reception in a separate chronological layer. The held Ezekiel/Revelation candidate stays held.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** High interpretive diversity; high curation complexity.

## 8. Micah 1 — Prophetic poetry, Assyrian horizon and textual witnesses

- **Current evidence:** 13 eligible items; 12 source-traceable placements. Current records include `ckl:micah#claim:micah-assyrian-horizon`, `#claim:micah-place-dirge`, `#claim:micah-sennacherib-material`, and `#claim:micah-textual-witnesses`. The claim `#claim:micah-empire-reception` is an unclassified but passage-anchored reception-history record.
- **Important context/question:** How does the oracle's eighth-century setting, place-name lament, and poetic sound-play function? What can Assyrian sources clarify, and which towns or routes remain uncertain? Which Dead Sea Scroll fragments actually preserve Micah text?
- **Underused evidence:** Textual witnesses are listed as sources on a claim rather than represented by a distinct manuscript-evidence item in the current chapter projection. Confirm verse extents before referring to 4Q81 or 4Q82 as witnesses to specific Micah readings.
- **Sources:** Primary text: Micah 1 in `micah-masoretic`; Assyrian annals and reliefs for imperial self-representation; and Dead Sea Scrolls only where the fragment actually preserves the verse under discussion. The [IAA archive records for 4Q81](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q81-1?locale=en_US) and [4Q82](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q82-1) are primary catalog references. Secondary sources: `kessler-micah`, `andersen-freedman-micah`, `catss-micah`, and `nets-twelve`.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Use this as a control for source chronology, textual-witness locators, uncertain geography, and a reception-history record that is currently not section-assigned.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** Moderate to high; moderate-to-high curation complexity.

## 9. Mark 2 — Gospel narrative, Sabbath and first-century Jewish debate

- **Current evidence:** 13 eligible items; 7 source-traceable placements. Existing records include `ckl:mark#claim:mark-jewish-debate`, anchored at Mark 2:23–28 and Mark 7:1–23, and `ckl:galilean-villages-households-and-subsistence#evidence:gospel-galilean-households`. Three items are unclassified, including `ckl:mark#claim:mark-disability` and `ckl:what-is-the-significance-of-the-sabbath`.
- **Important context/question:** What varieties of Sabbath practice and legal reasoning are visible in Second Temple Judaism? How does Mark frame these disputes and Jesus' authority? What do the healings and meal scenes contribute to the narrative sequence?
- **Underused evidence:** Primary text: Mark 2. The late-first-century-BCE 4Q271 Damascus Document preserves a sectarian rule collection with Sabbath statutes; the [Library of Congress exhibit](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/scr4.html) identifies its date and sectarian nature. It is a primary witness to one sectarian setting, not universal practice. Secondary sources include `jewish-annotated-nt`, `mark-food-purity`, `mark-religious-authorities`, `fiensy-strange-galilee`, and `chancey-galilee`.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Add or refine one carefully scoped example of a Second Temple comparison, explicitly labeling the Qumran text as one sectarian witness and distinguishing later rabbinic material. Do not present one group's rule as universal Jewish practice.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** Moderate; high curation complexity because broad social generalizations can mislead.

## 10. 1 Corinthians 11 — Epistle, assembly practice and contested interpretation

- **Current evidence:** 75 eligible items; 66 source-traceable placements; 2 unclassified items. The corpus already includes `ckl:1-corinthians#claim:1cor-kephale`, `#interpretive-note:sha256-4be5068e168f17d6c024d519a9a1c6f4a32060838091b75056ca746e1030466e`, `ckl:pauline-women-coworkers-prophecy-and-assembly-authority#claim:corinthian-kephale-dispute`, and `ckl:pauline-idols-sacrificed-food-market-temple-meals-conscience-knowledge-love-weak-strong-stumbling-participation-lords-supper-shared-table-and-economic-status#evidence:corinth-humiliating-have-nots`.
- **Important context/question:** What do *kephalē*, covering/hair practices, prophecy, honor, and socioeconomic divisions mean in the local assembly? How should the head-covering unit (11:2–16) and the communal meal (11:17–34) be related without making them one issue? What major scholarly options and textual evidence underlie disputed readings?
- **Underused evidence:** Primary text: 1 Corinthians 11 in `1cor-greek`; any specific Corinthian inscription or artifact must be separately identified before use. Current evidence uses sources such as `thiselton-1cor`, `wire-women`, `smith-banquet`, `friesen-poverty`, `payne-women`, and `ntvmr`. The American School of Classical Studies at Athens describes its [Corinth excavation and research publications](https://www.ascsa.org/excavations/ancient-corinth/research-publications) and open-access [Hesperia scholarship](https://ascsa.org/publications/hesperia/open-access); these can guide the secondary literature review.
- **Recommendation and benefit:** Treat as a high-density calibration pilot: audit alternatives, source locators, passage boundaries, terminology and section discoverability. Do not add more records merely because the chapter is controversial. Consult proprietary lexicons only under licensed access; do not copy their entries.
- **Uncertainty / complexity:** High; high curation complexity.
