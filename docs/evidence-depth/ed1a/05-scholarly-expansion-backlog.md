# ED-1A scholarly expansion backlog

Priorities combine reader value, current scoped CKL support, availability of traceable sources, historical/literary significance, reuse across passages, and the risk of misunderstanding. They are editorial research priorities, not additions authorized by this audit.

## Priority 1 — Psalm and wisdom literature

**Why now:** Psalms has 150 chapters: 74 have no eligible CKL item, only 6 have source-traceable historical/cultural types, only 2 have a source-traceable lexical claim, and 40 have a source-traceable literary-form record. Proverbs has 31 chapters: 12 are empty under passage projection, only 3 have source-traceable historical/cultural types, and none has a source-traceable primary-source or archaeology/geography tag. These books use poetry, proverbial instruction, acrostics, superscriptions, and royal/wisdom settings that can benefit from careful context without requiring an archaeological entry in every chapter.

**First research targets:** Psalm 72 (4 eligible items, 2 source-traceable child placements), Psalm 82 (existing material and a held relationship candidate that must remain held), and Proverbs 31 (6 eligible items, 5 traceable, including a source-backed acrostic claim). Separate an ancient audience question from later reception; do not infer a date from a superscription alone. Start with existing sources such as `psalms-masoretic`, `oxford-psalter-metanarrative`, `declaisse-walford-jacobson-tanner`, `goldingay-psalms`, `proverbs-masoretic`, `fox-proverbs-ten-thirty-one`, and `cambridge-proverbs-thirty-one`.

**Reader value:** Poetry and wisdom become more legible as literary acts situated in ancient worship, kingship, household economies, and instruction, while interpretation remains qualified.

## Priority 2 — Chronicler's historical and literary setting

**Why now:** 2 Chronicles has 14 of 36 chapters with no eligible item, only 1 chapter with a source-traceable historical/cultural typed record, and 2 with an ancient-primary-source typed record. 1 Chronicles has 3 of 29 chapters with no eligible item. 2 Chronicles 30 has 9 eligible items but only 3 source-traceable child placements.

**First research target:** 2 Chronicles 30: Hezekiah's Passover, the narrative's account of northern participation, ritual timing, and its relationship to the preceding reform narrative and Kings. Existing sources include `2-chronicles-1-36`, `klein-second-chronicles`, and (for the surrounding Assyrian/Samaria setting) `radner-israel-house-omri` and `cogan-binding-samaria-2022`. No contemporary independent primary source for this particular Passover celebration is identified by this audit. Do not present the Sennacherib campaign as direct proof of the festival narrative.

**Reader value:** Better distinction among Chronicler's narrative, historical setting, theological presentation, and later communal identity.

## Priority 3 — Ezekiel's restoration visions

**Why now:** Ezekiel has 9 of 48 chapters without eligible CKL material; only 4 chapters have source-traceable historical/cultural types, 5 have primary-source types, and none has a source-traceable archaeology/geography type. This signals an opportunity for historical and literary orientation, not an archaeology quota.

**First research target:** Ezekiel 40: the vision's exile setting, literary genre, internal measurements and spatial sequence, priestly roles, and later reception. Existing sources include `ezekiel-masoretic`, `block-ezekiel-25-48`, `ganzel-priests-prince`, and `sulzbach-future-map`. Keep the Ezekiel 40–48 / Revelation 21–22 bridge held under NE-10; a shared temple/city image does not authorize a route or identify a future building.

**Reader value:** Readers can follow the vision's own ancient literary world without flattening it into an archaeological plan or a single later theological reading.

## Priority 4 — Assyrian-period source literacy and domain links

**Why now:** 1 Kings has 6 of 22 chapters with no eligible item and no chapters with source-traceable ancient-primary-source or archaeology/geography typed records. 2 Kings has 6 of 25 empty, but 2 Kings 18 is a strong control case (34 eligible items, 21 source-traceable child placements, including 6 primary-source and 9 archaeology/geography placements).

**First research target:** Use 2 Kings 18 (and Isaiah 36–37 as a separate literary witness) to document how to compare biblical narrative, Assyrian annals, and Lachish reliefs without collapsing distinct royal accounts. Current records include `ckl:sennacherib-prism#evidence:taylor-prism-annals`, `ckl:sennacherib-prism#evidence:lachish-relief-royal-siege-rhetoric`, and `ckl:assyrian-tribute-and-royal-representation#evidence:sennacherib-hezekiah-tribute`. The British Museum's [Taylor Prism record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1855-1003-1) and its [Lachish relief catalog](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1856-0909-14_5) are accessible collection records. Preserve Assyrian royal ideology, biblical narrative, event date, and archaeological context as distinct descriptions.

**Reader value:** Historical sources add texture while making their purpose, perspective, and limits visible.

## Priority 5 — Passage-specific original-language work

**Why now:** The packaged lexicon offers 14,197 entries and 444,339 aligned verse-word rows, but only 107 CKL claims are typed lexical; only 140 source-traceable lexical record placements occur across 100 chapters. The service supports investigation; it does not do passage-specific research for the reader.

**First research targets:** Review lexical questions only when morphology, syntax, idiom, semantic range, or translation materially affects the passage. High-value examples already surfaced include `ckl:1-corinthians#claim:1cor-kephale` (1 Corinthians 11:3), `corinthian-kephale-dispute`, and `corinth-examine-discern-body`. Genesis 14's `g14-hebrew-designation-ivri` is typed as worldview evidence rather than a lexical claim; assess whether that is the intended authoring distinction before changing any record.

**Reader value:** Readers can distinguish a dictionary gloss from an argument about a word in context. Do not copy proprietary definitions; consult licensed works for research and author original concise explanations with exact locators.

## Priority 6 — Eighth-century Judah, Micah, and textual witnesses

**Why now:** Micah currently has eligible CKL items in all 7 chapters and source-traceable historical/cultural types in all 7; this is an existing high-value cluster that can establish an audit standard. Micah 1 has 13 eligible items and 12 source-traceable placements.

**First research target:** Review `ckl:micah#claim:micah-assyrian-horizon`, `micah-place-dirge`, `micah-sennacherib-material`, and `micah-textual-witnesses`. Sources already listed include `kessler-micah`, `british-museum-prism`, `british-museum-lachish`, `catss-micah`, `iaa-4q81`, and `iaa-4q82`. Confirm exactly which fragments preserve Micah readings before describing them as witnesses to a specific verse. Keep uncertain site identifications and the poetic place-name dirge distinct from a route reconstructed from coordinates.

**Reader value:** A clear example of how eighth-century imperial history, poetic rhetoric, geography, and textual witnesses can be connected without overstating any one source.

## Priority 7 — Second Temple setting in Gospel passages

**Why now:** The Gospel corpus has better general historical coverage than Psalms/Chronicles, but chapter-level questions still require precise evidence. Mark has source-traceable historical/cultural types in 11 of 16 chapters and ancient-primary-source types in 5; Mark 2 has 13 eligible items, 7 source-traceable placements, and 3 unclassified items.

**First research target:** Mark 2: clarify the Sabbath disputes, meal associations, purity and authority questions in their first-century Jewish setting. Review `ckl:mark#claim:mark-jewish-debate` and `ckl:galilean-villages-households-and-subsistence#evidence:gospel-galilean-households`. The late-first-century-BCE 4Q271 Damascus Document fragment contains sectarian Sabbath statutes (Library of Congress [Damascus Document exhibit](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/scr4.html)); it is one community's rule collection, not a universal Jewish code and not a direct parallel to every action in Mark 2. Later rabbinic compilations should be labeled later reception.

**Reader value:** Avoid portraying diverse Jewish practices as a single uniform background or describing Jesus' opponents as representatives of all Judaism.

## Priority 8 — Source-identity and locator review

**Why now:** Two unresolved source IDs, one missing expected locator, 558 source IDs below a minimal bibliography threshold, and 81 source IDs with conflicting non-empty identity metadata create friction for any later reuse. Manual spot checks confirm same-ID records that appear to conflate distinct publications: `aune` includes both *Revelation* and *The New Testament in Its Literary Environment*; `bauckham` includes *Jude, 2 Peter* and *The Theology of the Book of Revelation*; `burnett-imperial-honors` spans different authors and publications. These are concrete source-identity candidates, unlike locator variation alone.

**Action:** Create a source-owner review list from the exact IDs and field values in `summary.json`; first confirm whether a stable source ID denotes one publication, one edition, one series, or a catalog record. The one missing locator is `patristic-minor-prophets`. Do not modify citations or canonical source locks from an audit report alone.

## Priority 9 — Section assignment and preview discoverability

**Why now:** 1,023 projected CKL item placements in 394 chapters are not assigned to a Narrative Explorer section. The examples `ckl:1-corinthians#claim:1cor-kephale` and `ckl:micah#claim:micah-empire-reception` are passage-specific typed records that merit a consumer-path review. Not all unclassified items are section candidates; Proverbs 31's `ckl:proverbs#claim:proverbs-greek-order` does not have its own anchor in that chapter.

**Action:** In a future bounded implementation review, trace exact stable identities through section assignment and evidence preview eligibility. Keep category semantics and the preview selector intact unless the audit shows a real classification mismatch. Do not add new CKL records to compensate for a display problem.

## Priority 10 — Explicit archaeology and map integration triage

**Why now:** Four archaeology service items have no valid passage link and 57 map-place catalog entries have no valid link, while all stored current references and targets pass validation.

**Action:** Ask domain owners whether each catalog item actually applies to a passage before linking. Preserve the archaeology database as catalog authority. Do not derive a biblical site identity from coordinates or infer that a map or archaeological item is required for every chapter.
