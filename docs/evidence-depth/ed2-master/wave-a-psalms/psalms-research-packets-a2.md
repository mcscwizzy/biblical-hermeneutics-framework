## Psalm 5 — does “in the morning” establish a temple ritual?

- **Existing CKL:** no passage-eligible child item. The broad Psalter source inventory is retained.
- **Research question:** Does the morning petition identify a precise temple ceremony or time of prayer?
- **Textual observation:** In Hebrew numbering, the speaker lays a request before YHWH “in the morning” and waits (v. 4; English/ASV v. 3); the poem later invokes entry to YHWH’s house (Hebrew v. 8; English v. 7) and contrasts the wicked with those who take refuge (Hebrew vv. 5–7, 10–13; English vv. 4–6, 9–12). The CKL anchor follows English/ASV numbering: Psalm 5:1–12.
- **Verified source:** Philippus J. Botha, “Psalm 5 and the polarity between those who may stand before Yahweh and those who may not,” *HTS Teologiese Studies / Theological Studies* 74, no. 1 (2018), article a5087, introductory discussion of v. 4 and “Textual and literary analysis of Psalm 5.” [DOI/full open article](https://doi.org/10.4102/hts.v74i1.5087). Full article inspected.
- **Interpretation and uncertainty:** Botha describes a traditional legal-prayer/morning-sacrifice reading but cautions against inferring precise time and temple location from the terse wording. His own David/Absalom and post-exilic-community reading concerns the psalm’s present literary placement and is a proposal, not an established original setting.
- **Record:** `psalm-5-morning-motif-and-setting-limits`.
- **Reader relevance:** Separates explicit morning and temple language from a reconstructed ceremony or historical occasion.

## Psalm 20 — Papyrus Amherst 63 and the relationship’s direction

- **Existing CKL:** no passage-eligible child item. No authored cross-passage relationship is added.
- **Research question:** Does the Aramaic prayer in Papyrus Amherst 63 establish the origin or direction of literary dependence for Psalm 20?
- **Textual observation:** Papyrus Amherst 63, col. XII, lines 11–19, preserves an Aramaic prayer written in Demotic script. Its petitions to be answered, helped, and granted requests correspond to portions of Psalm 20:1–5 in common English numbering (MT 20:2–6). The papyrus has a distinct divine-name repertoire and references such as Zaphon and Bethel.
- **Verified sources:** Stephen Elliott Ward, *Examining the Judean Scribal Tendencies in the Psalms of Papyrus Amherst 63, with Col. XII, 11–19 as a Test Case* (PhD diss., University of California, Los Angeles, 2023), ch. 3, pp. 81–109, especially 82–83; discussion of earlier proposals, pp. 20–22. [Full dissertation](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4mg6x6q4/qt4mg6x6q4_noSplash_2c33e63fa5ce42bc2069cca24a13f3da.pdf). Full text inspected. Menahem Kister, “Psalm 20 and Papyrus Amherst 63: A window to the dynamic nature of poetic texts,” *Vetus Testamentum* 70, no. 3 (2020), 426–457. [Institutional bibliographic record and abstract](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/psalm-20-and-papyrus-amherst-63-a-window-to-the-dynamic-nature-of/). Only the record and abstract were accessible; the full article was not consulted.
- **Interpretation and uncertainty:** Ward surveys competing histories: Nims and Steiner treat the papyrus text as a paganized adaptation of Psalm 20, while other scholarship proposes that the biblical psalm reoriented an earlier northern/Samarian prayer. The Demotic–Aramaic script/language combination complicates transcription, divine-name identification, and translation. The verified parallel does not settle direction, direct copying, community provenance, or Psalm 20’s date.
- **Record:** `psalm-20-amherst63-parallel-and-direction-debate`.
- **Reader relevance:** Presents a concrete ancient literary parallel while distinguishing an extrabiblical text from a manuscript witness to the Hebrew psalm.

## Psalm 25 — individual lament and the final appeal for Israel

- **Existing CKL:** no passage-eligible child item.
- **Research question:** How does the final collective petition relate to the preceding individual acrostic prayer?
- **Textual observation:** First-person petitions and reflections structure vv. 1–21; v. 22 broadens the plea to Israel.
- **Verified source:** D. J. Human, “The tradition-historical setting of Psalm 25: How wisdom motives contribute to its understanding,” *Verbum et Ecclesia* 17, no. 1 (1996), 76–88, structure and genre, 78–79, and conclusion, 85–86. [DOI/full article](https://doi.org/10.4102/ve.v17i1.1112). Full article inspected.
- **Interpretation and uncertainty:** Human classifies the poem as an individual lament and calls v. 22 an epilogue that extends the individual experience to Israel. He treats later addition as possible, not certain, and notes that scholars divide the stanzas differently. Neither the acrostic nor the collective ending dates the poem.
- **Record:** `psalm-25-individual-prayer-and-collective-epilogue`.
- **Reader relevance:** Makes the transition from personal petition to communal appeal visible without collapsing the final verse into a settled redaction history.
