# Wave A research packets — A8

Batch A8 reviews six Psalms that had no eligible passage-specific CKL material in the baseline census. Existing broad book or entity context is not duplicated. Each item adds one narrowly framed literary question and remains draft/in-review for human review.

## Psalm 70 — concentric structure proposal

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-specific item in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** Does a proposed concentric pattern help readers follow the short repeated plea for deliverance?
- **Source and locator:** Pierre Auffret, “A mon aide hâte-toi ! Nouvelle étude structurelle du Psaume 70,” *Old Testament Essays* 24/1 (2011), 284–289; English and French abstracts in the [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.10520/EJC86085). Only the abstracts were accessible; the full French article was not reviewed.
- **Textual observation:** BHF Psalm 70 has five body verses. The opening and closing ask God to hurry to help; the middle turns toward those who seek God and rejoice.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** The abstract reports Auffret’s proposal of a concentric outline around his verse 4, with verses 3/5 and then 2/6 corresponding. His verse numbering includes the superscription: the proposed center maps to BHF v. 3, the first pair to vv. 2/4, and the outer pair to vv. 1/5.
- **Uncertainty/access:** Abstract-only. No judgment is made about whether this outline is better than prior proposals, and no historical use or composition date is inferred.
- **CKL record:** `psalm-70-concentric-structure-proposal`.
- **Reader relevance:** Offers a testable form proposal without presenting it as a settled structure.

## Psalm 86 — prayer within Psalms 84–88

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-specific item in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** How might the surrounding Korahite psalms shape the final-form reading of Psalm 86’s Davidic prayer?
- **Source and locator:** James Alfred Loader, “Levels of Contextual Synergy in the Korah Psalms: The Example of Psalm 86,” *Old Testament Essays* 23/3 (2010), 666–680, especially sections C–D, pp. 668–675. [Full open-access article](https://www.scielo.org.za/pdf/ote/v23n3/09.pdf) inspected.
- **Textual observation:** Psalm 86 carries a Davidic heading and is placed between Psalms 84–85 and 87–88, whose headings identify a surrounding Korahite sequence.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Loader describes a 2+1+2 genre pattern: individual praise, collective lament, Psalm 86 as central individual lament, Zion praise, and individual lament. He argues the placement adds a contextual dimension of hope to the prayer.
- **Uncertainty:** This is a final-form and redactional reading. The superscription does not independently prove historical authorship by David, a historical speaker, or an exile-era composition.
- **CKL record:** `psalm-86-korah-collection-lament-frame`.
- **Reader relevance:** Helps readers consider a psalm’s collected placement while distinguishing it from its immediate poetic voice.

## Psalm 87 — Zion’s status and the named nations

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-specific item in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** Do Rahab, Babylon, Philistia, Tyre, and Cush indicate inclusion in Zion or sharpen Zion’s distinct status?
- **Source and locator:** Nicolene Rautenbach, “YHWH Loves Zion – Zion Loves YHWH: An Exploration of the Workings of Ancient Near Eastern Social Values in Psalm 87,” *Old Testament Essays* 21/2 (2008), 422–434, especially sections C–G, pp. 424–432. [Full University of Pretoria repository copy](https://repository.up.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/04f92d83-467c-45fd-af9a-f8ad87cec6cb/content) inspected.
- **Textual observation:** The poem praises YHWH’s love for Zion over Jacob’s other dwellings, names several foreign peoples, and repeats a formula about being born there.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Rautenbach uses the Korahite context and proposed patronage, group-identity, and honour values to argue that the names emphasize Zion’s special status rather than equal citizenship for all peoples. Her article also surveys readings that understand the nations as included.
- **Uncertainty:** The poem’s structure and the force of its birth formula are contested. Rautenbach notes that her published article began as an MA seminar paper; its social-value model and exclusivity reading are attributed proposals, not archaeological findings or consensus.
- **CKL record:** `psalm-87-zion-status-reading-dispute`.
- **Reader relevance:** Makes an interpretive dispute visible and helps readers separate the text’s ancient literary rhetoric from later universalizing reception.

## Psalm 98 — proposed kingship group in Book IV

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-specific item in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** How might Psalm 98’s creation-wide praise function within the proposed YHWH-kingship sequence in Psalms 93–100?
- **Source and locator:** Jorge Blunda Grubert, “El reinado de Yhwh anticipado en la liturgia de Israel. Sal 93–100 como composición,” *Salmanticensis* 67/1 (2020), 11–34; Spanish abstract in the [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.36576/summa.128664). The full PDF was not accessible.
- **Textual observation:** Psalm 98 celebrates divine victory and justice, addresses nations and earth, and summons sea, rivers, and mountains to praise.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** The abstract presents Psalms 93–100 as a proposed composition centered on YHWH’s kingship and specifically names Psalm 98 in a study of spatial and temporal structure.
- **Uncertainty/access:** Abstract-only. No detailed literary argument, historical date, or specific liturgical performance was verified.
- **CKL record:** `psalm-98-yhwh-kingship-in-book-four-cluster`.
- **Reader relevance:** Offers a focused question about the psalm’s collected setting while preventing the abstract’s mention of liturgy from becoming an invented event.

## Psalm 101 — royal voice and public ethics

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-specific item in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** How does the poem connect a ruler’s personal integrity to public justice and conduct in the city?
- **Source and locator:** Philippus J. Botha, “Psalm 101: A supplication for the restoration of society in the late post-exilic age,” *HTS Teologiese Studies / Theological Studies* 72/4 (2016), article a3389; sections “Form and purpose of Psalm 101” and “Psalm 101 in Book IV,” especially PDF pp. 1–7. [Full open-access article](https://doi.org/10.4102/hts.v72i4.3389) inspected.
- **Textual observation:** The speaker sings of steadfast love and justice, vows to walk with integrity, rejects slander and deceit, and closes with a repeated-morning judgment of evildoers.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Botha reads vv. 1–4 as personal integrity and vv. 5–8 as public/royal conduct, and proposes a wisdom/Torah-shaped Book IV prayer for just rule.
- **Uncertainty:** Botha proposes a late Persian or early Hellenistic setting and critiques corrupt Judean leadership, but surveys divergent readings of genre, unity, segmentation, and date. No named king, enthronement ceremony, or historical crisis is established.
- **CKL record:** `psalm-101-royal-voice-and-community-justice`.
- **Reader relevance:** Connects household and public conduct in the poem without treating its royal voice as a transcript of an actual ruler.

## Psalm 108 — head imagery and poetic victory

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-specific item in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** How does body/head language function within the poem’s territorial oracle and plea for divine aid?
- **Source and locator:** Lodewyk Sutton and D. J. Human, “‘Off with their heads!’ The imagery of the head in the trilogy of Psalms 108–110 (Part 2),” *Stellenbosch Theological Journal* 3/1 (2017), sections 3.1 and 4. [Full open-access article](https://doi.org/10.17570/stj.2017.v3n1.a19) inspected.
- **Textual observation:** The oracle assigns territories, describes Ephraim as a helmet and Judah as a ruler’s staff, and the poem closes by confessing that victory comes from God.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Sutton and Human read head imagery as warfare language expressing honour and shame and treat Psalms 108–110 as a literary sequence of rescue petition, conflict, and victory.
- **Uncertainty:** Their sequence, social-scientific framing, and proposed post-exilic royal horizon are attributed readings; they do not identify a specific campaign. Their numbering counts the superscription: their Psalm 108:9 and 14 correspond to BHF body vv. 8 and 13.
- **CKL record:** `psalm-108-head-imagery-and-royal-victory-metaphor`.
- **Reader relevance:** Helps readers notice the metaphorical force of conquest language without turning poetic claims into a historical battle report.
