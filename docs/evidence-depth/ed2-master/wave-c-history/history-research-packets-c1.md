## 2 Chronicles 14:9–15 — the Cushite army and Asa's battle account

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-anchored claim or typed evidence item for 2 Chronicles 14.
- **Research question:** What does the account's description of Zerah's force establish, and why does Chronicles include a Cushite battle not present in Kings' Asa account?
- **Verified source:** Louis C. Jonker, “The Cushites in Herodotus and Chronicles: Revisiting the Asa Narrative,” *Old Testament Essays* 36, no. 1 (2023), 89–105, especially pp. 99–100. The full open-access article was inspected in the journal-linked SciELO text.
- **Textual observation:** Chronicles names Zerah as a Cushite and describes a very large army and chariots. The Kings account of Asa's reign does not narrate this battle; Chronicles 16:8 later recalls Cushites and Libyans.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Jonker reads the army figures as metaphorical language for the disparity of force, not as an exact count. He proposes that the Cushite episode serves a rhetorical function in Chronicles. He treats the composition horizon and the lack of a Kings parallel as parts of that reconstruction, not as proof that no conflict occurred.
- **Uncertainty:** Zerah's identification and the historical referent of the episode remain disputed. The source does not make the biblical troop figures an independently verified census or establish a particular archaeological event.
- **Record:** `2chronicles-14-cushites-and-rhetorical-setting`.
- **Reader relevance:** Separates the narrative's theological description of an overwhelming force from precise military history and later identifications of Zerah.

## 2 Chronicles 18:1–34 — Micaiah, Ahab, and Jehoshaphat's alliance

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-anchored claim or typed evidence item for 2 Chronicles 18.
- **Research question:** Why does the Chronicler retain and reframe the Micaiah narrative shared with Kings?
- **Verified source:** Ray Dillard, “The Chronicler's Jehoshaphat,” *Trinity Journal* 7, no. 1 (1986), 17–22, especially pp. 20–21. The full article text was inspected; the host identifies it as reproduced by permission.
- **Textual observation:** The Ramoth-gilead account appears in both 1 Kings 22:1–40 and 2 Chronicles 18:1–34. Chronicles follows it with the rebuke of Jehoshaphat in 19:1–3.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Dillard argues that Kings mainly uses the episode to account for Ahab's death, while Chronicles places it within Jehoshaphat's moral evaluation and the rebuke that follows. He also sees the Chronicler patterning Jehoshaphat's reign after Asa's. This is a literary-theological reading of the biblical narratives, not independent event verification.
- **Uncertainty:** Dillard's account is one scholarly interpretation. The sequence and parallel wording are textual observations; the Chronicler's purpose is a literary inference. No composition date is asserted.
- **Record:** `2chronicles-18-jehoshaphat-and-micaiah`.
- **Reader relevance:** Helps readers compare the narrative function of the same account across Kings and Chronicles while seeing how Chronicles' following verdict changes its emphasis.

## 2 Chronicles 25:1–28 — Amaziah in Kings and Chronicles

- **Existing CKL material:** No eligible passage-anchored claim or typed evidence item for 2 Chronicles 25.
- **Research question:** How does the Chronicler's version of Amaziah's reign differ from the parallel account in Kings, and what interpretation does Hwang propose?
- **Verified source:** Hwang Sunwoo, “The Chronicler's Intention Revealed in the Comparison of the Amaziah Accounts between Kings and Chronicles,” *Evangelical Old Testament Studies* 32 (2025), 62–93, DOI 10.22782/eots.2025.32..003. KCI metadata and its English abstract were inspected; the full article is in Korean and was not consulted.
- **Textual observation:** Hwang's abstract identifies 2 Kings 14:1–20 as the parallel to 2 Chronicles 25:1–28 and reports that Chronicles follows some material while changing or omitting other sections and adding distinctive material.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** The abstract says Hwang reads these changes as emphasizing retributive justice, narrative continuity, Amaziah's sin, and the legitimacy of Judah under the Davidic covenant. This item is low-confidence and reports only the abstract; it does not claim to assess the detailed argument.
- **Uncertainty/access limit:** The full article and its verse-level comparisons were not verified. No exact historical reconstruction or composition date is inferred from the abstract.
- **Record:** `2chronicles-25-amaziah-and-kings-parallel`.
- **Reader relevance:** Invites direct comparison of Kings and Chronicles and makes clear that theological claims about the Chronicler remain attributed scholarship.
