# Wave C research packet — C2

C2 examines a literary and collection-history question in the Chronicler’s presentation of Davidic worship. The addition remains draft/in-review and requires human scholarly review.

## 1 Chronicles 16:36 — the Psalm 106 doxology

- **Existing CKL material:** First Chronicles 16 already has two broad or minimally useful records about the ark and Davidic worship, but it had no typed passage-specific evidence item for the closing liturgical response.
- **Research question:** Does the reuse of Psalm 106:48 in 1 Chronicles 16:36 provide evidence that the Chronicler knew a five-book Psalter?
- **Verified sources:** Drew Longacre, “When Was the Psalter Compiled?,” *The Bible and Interpretation*, September 2025, “Chronicles” section. The complete essay was inspected; the site identifies Longacre as a University of Münster research fellow. This is a scholarly web essay, not a peer-reviewed journal article. The Masoretic Psalm 106 is also included as the direct textual comparison.
- **Textual observation:** First Chronicles 16:36 closes with the people saying “Amen” and praising YHWH; the wording closely matches Psalm 106:48. The surrounding hymn also uses material found in Psalms 105, 96, and 106.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Longacre reads Psalm 106:48 as the doxology closing book four and argues that its reuse in the Chronicler’s hymn, with other Psalm material, supports a five-book Psalter known to the Chronicler. He acknowledges debate over whether the doxology originally belonged to Psalm 106 itself, the exact date of Chronicles, and the possibility that some individual psalms were incorporated later.
- **Uncertainty/access limit:** The source is a scholarly essay rather than a peer-reviewed article. Its collection-history and Persian-period chronology argument is reported as Longacre’s proposal, not consensus or direct evidence of the narrated Davidic performance.
- **Record:** `first-chronicles-16-36-five-book-psalter`.
- **Reader relevance:** Helps readers connect a specific liturgical response to the history of the Psalter without assuming that the Chronicler’s Davidic setting is a verbatim historical transcript.
