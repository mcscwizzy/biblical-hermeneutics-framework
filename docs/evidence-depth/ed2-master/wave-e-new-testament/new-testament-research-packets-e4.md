## John 10:22 — “the Dedication” and the winter notice

- **Existing CKL material:** John 10 already has passage-specific material, but no typed evidence item addressed the Greek festival expression in verse 22 or its seasonal note.
- **Research question:** Does the Greek expression τὰ ἐγκαίνια itself name Hanukkah, or does John’s note that it was winter carry the identifying information?
- **Verified source:** Hans Förster, “Zur Bedeutung von ἐγκαίνια in Joh 10,22,” *Revue Biblique* 123, no. 3 (2016), 400–417, DOI 10.2143/RBI.123.3.3159683. The publisher metadata and abstract were inspected; the full article was inaccessible.
- **Textual observation:** John 10:22 uses τὰ ἐγκαίνια and explicitly adds that it was winter; the Greek text does not transliterate the English festival label “Hanukkah.”
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Förster argues from the abstract that the Greek expression is a generic “celebration of a dedication,” and that the winter notice helps identify the occasion as Hanukkah among possible temple-dedication celebrations.
- **Uncertainty/access limit:** Only the abstract supports the scholarly argument. The record does not independently survey first-century terminology, adopt another temple dedication as the setting, or infer the Gospel’s audience composition from the seasonal note.
- **Record:** `john-10-22-dedication-term-and-winter`.
- **Reader relevance:** Shows how a short seasonal note can carry historical and translation significance while marking the limits of an abstract-level source review.
