## Mark 7:3 — πυγμῇ or πυκνά?

- **Existing CKL material:** Mark already has broad material on handwashing, purity, and disputes over Mark 7:19. The existing records do not address the translation crux in 7:3, the Greek form at issue here.
- **Research question:** Does the difficult phrase in Mark 7:3 support the principal critical-text reading πυγμῇ (“with a fist”), or should readers consider the variant πυκνά (“repeatedly”)?
- **Verified source:** Delbert Burkett, “Did Jews Rinse their Hands ‘with a Fist’ (Mark 7.3)?,” *New Testament Studies* 65, no. 2 (2019), 139–147, DOI 10.1017/S0028688518000358. Cambridge’s page verifies the author, title, journal, issue, pages, DOI, and abstract; its displayed footnote 1 lists manuscript and versional forms. The complete article was not accessible. The SBL Greek New Testament was consulted for the verse’s Greek text.
- **Original-context evidence:** Mark places the phrase in an explanation of a handwashing practice during a dispute about eating bread with unwashed hands. The record makes no general claim about how all Jews washed or about the normativity of this practice.
- **Textual observation:** Burkett reports that NA28 and UBS5 print πυγμῇ and proposes the variant πυκνά. His displayed footnote lists א and W among witnesses for πυκνά; no primary manuscript images were inspected, and the complete witness discussion was inaccessible.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Burkett argues that πυκνά, understood as “repeatedly,” better fits accounts of repeated rinsing in later rabbinic literature. The item reports this as his proposal and does not identify the original reading. The later texts he invokes are not treated as direct proof of a uniform first-century practice.
- **Major uncertainty/access limit:** Only the abstract and displayed apparatus note were accessible. The article’s full textual argument, complete witness discussion, and facsimiles remain unreviewed. The item is low confidence and requires full-text and manuscript-apparatus review before any reading could be preferred.
- **Record:** `mark-7-3-pugme-pukna-variant`.
- **Reader relevance:** Shows how a small Greek reading can change an English translation and where the evidence remains unresolved, without turning a debated phrase into a claim about Jewish practice as a whole.
