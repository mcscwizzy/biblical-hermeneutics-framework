## Proverbs 6:1–5 — surety and the referent of “stranger”

- **Existing CKL material:** No passage-anchored claim, typed evidence item, or passage-specific interpretive note was eligible for Proverbs 6 in the baseline census.
- **Research question:** Does the “stranger” in Proverbs 6:1 identify a foreigner, and what can be said about the surety arrangement described?
- **Verified source:** R. N. Whybray, *The Book of Proverbs* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1972), 94–95, especially the note on zār on p. 95. The scanned commentary passage was inspected in full. Cambridge University Press front matter confirms author, title, publisher, and year.
- **Textual observation:** The warning begins with conditional clauses and continues with urgent imperatives. Verse 1 uses the surety verb ʿārab, the phrase tāqaʿ kaph (“strike hands”), and the paired terms rēaʿ (“neighbor”) and zār (“stranger”).
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Whybray reads ʿārab as a guarantee to a creditor on another person’s behalf and regards “striking hands” as a pledge-related ceremony. He takes Proverbs 22:27 to indicate a financial pledge. For zār, he reports Boström’s “foreigner” reading and Snijders’s “outsider” reading; Whybray prefers an Israelite outside one’s family and says the text does not support a foreign-trade scenario.
- **Uncertainty:** Whybray notes that the Hebrew syntax and the relation between “neighbor” and “stranger” are ambiguous. This packet reports his preferred reconstruction and alternatives; it does not establish an Israelite legal procedure, a specific economic arrangement, or an actual transaction. Composition date remains unestablished.
- **Record:** `proverbs-6-surety-and-stranger`.
- **Reader relevance:** Keeps the warning’s financial and relational terms visible while preventing an uncertain word from being turned into a confident claim about foreign commerce.
