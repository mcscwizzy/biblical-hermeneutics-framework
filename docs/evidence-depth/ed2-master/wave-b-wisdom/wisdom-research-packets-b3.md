## Proverbs 15:25–16:9 — Hebrew and Greek textual arrangement

- **Existing CKL material:** The book has broad contextual records and passage-specific material in Proverbs 2, 6, and 28, but no eligible passage-specific item in chapters 15 or 16.
- **Research question:** Why do the Masoretic and Old Greek forms arrange Proverbs 15:25–16:9 differently, and what can the surviving Qumran fragment establish?
- **Verified source:** Jean-Daniel Macchi, “« De Yhwh la réponse de la langue ». Étude sur Proverbes 15,25–16,9,” *Études Théologiques et Religieuses* 99, no. 2 (2024), 381–394, DOI 10.3917/etr.992.0381. The full published French article was inspected in the University of Geneva repository, especially pp. 382–383 for versional differences and 4QProvb.
- **Textual observation:** MT Proverbs 16:1–3 and 15:31 have no Old Greek equivalents. Several Greek lines at 15:27a, 28a, and 29a–b correspond to MT 16:6–9. Macchi notes that 4QProvb preserves only part of the section and supports the MT order in its overlap.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** Macchi argues that a proto-Masoretic editor reshaped a Hebrew form underlying the Old Greek by relocating lines and composing MT 16:1–3, emphasizing divine sovereignty and preparing for the royal sayings of 16:10–15. He reviews alternatives involving translation freedom and multiple Hebrew editions; his proposal is not a directly dated event.
- **Uncertainty/access limit:** The article is full-text and in French. The record attributes its preferred text-history reconstruction, does not assign a date, and does not treat the fragmentary Qumran witness as settling the history of the whole passage.
- **Record:** `proverbs-15-25-to-16-9-mt-lxx-redaction-debate`.
- **Reader relevance:** The comparison alerts readers that a familiar chapter sequence reflects one textual form and that ancient witnesses preserve a different arrangement.
