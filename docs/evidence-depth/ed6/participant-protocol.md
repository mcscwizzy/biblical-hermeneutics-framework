# Participant Protocol

## Purpose and session length

This is exploratory usability research about the BHF reader and the way it
shows contextual research and evidence. It is not a test of biblical knowledge,
faith, spiritual maturity, or doctrinal agreement. A session should take about
20–30 minutes, including consent and a short debrief.

## Participants and recruitment

Aim for 6–8 consenting adults. Recruit beyond current BHF users and include a
mix of people who regularly study Scripture, people with basic familiarity, and
people unfamiliar with scholarly biblical research. Invite at least two mobile
users if feasible. Do not recruit only experienced BHF users.

Use broad, optional descriptions of study experience if they help balance the
sample: regular, some, limited or none, or prefer not to say. Do not ask for a
denomination, church, religious belief, membership, or personal testimony. Do
not retain names or contact details in research notes. Arrange sessions through
the facilitator's ordinary contact channel and keep any scheduling information
outside the research dataset.

## Session sequence

| Time | Activity |
|---:|---|
| 0–3 min | Read the [consent script](consent-and-privacy.md); answer questions and record yes/no only. |
| 3–5 min | Explain think-aloud, select the participant's familiar passage, and open BHF. |
| 5–22 min | Complete four task cards, allowing about 3–4 minutes each. Participants may stop or skip at any time. |
| 22–27 min | Ask the [neutral post-session questions](post-session-questions.md). |
| 27–30 min | Thank the participant, remind them how notes are stored, and close without interpreting their answers. |

Use the assignment order in [study-task-bank.md](study-task-bank.md). Each
participant receives four tasks: one self-selected familiar-passage discovery
task, the common Deuteronomy 32 verification task, and two additional tasks
from a balanced passage group. The six-session order includes the 1 Kings 2
negative case twice. If a participant stops early, preserve only the
observations actually made and mark the session incomplete or stopped.

## Study conduct

Read task prompts as written. Do not teach a passage, point to the relevant
button, paraphrase the evidence, or explain an interpretation before the
participant has a chance to explore. Use a neutral clarification only when a
task instruction or physical control is unclear. See the [facilitator guide](facilitator-guide.md)
for exact prompts and note-taking boundaries.

Participation is voluntary. A participant may decline a task, skip a question,
or stop the session at any time without giving a reason. No BHF account,
analytics tracking, screen or voice recording, or external upload is required.

## Results and limits

Report observed task counts with the denominator for each measure. Preserve
partial sessions and missing answers as not observed. A small convenience
sample describes the people who took part; it is not a statistically
representative estimate of all Bible readers. Do not treat an interpretation
that differs from a facilitator's view as a task failure.
