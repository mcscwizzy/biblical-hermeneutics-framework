# Facilitator Guide

## Before the session

- Read the [protocol](participant-protocol.md) and [consent script](consent-and-privacy.md).
- Select one four-task assignment from the [task bank](study-task-bank.md).
- Prepare the notes template with an anonymous code only after consent.
- Open BHF in a clean browser session. Do not sign in as a participant or
  enable tracking. For a mobile participant, use their own device where
  practical and record only `mobile` as the device class.
- Keep the task prompts visible, but do not show the facilitator-only evidence
  IDs or source answers.

## Opening the session

Read the consent script without adding a preferred interpretation. Say:

> I will ask you to explore a few passages. Please think aloud: tell me what
> you are looking for, what you expect a control to do, and what you notice.
> There are no right answers for this session. I am observing how the reader
> works for you. You can stop or skip anything.

Ask permission before taking notes, and confirm separate quote consent before
writing a direct quotation. Do not ask the participant to explain their
religious identity or beliefs.

## Think-aloud and neutral clarification

Use neutral prompts only when the participant becomes silent or asks what to
do next:

- “What are you looking for right now?”
- “What do you expect this control to do?”
- “What does that label mean to you?”
- “What would you try next?”
- “What on the screen led you to that conclusion?”

If a control is physically unclear, clarify only the action, for example:
“That button opens the research panel.” Do not point to a passage answer, name
the relevant evidence, explain a manuscript reading, or tell the participant
which interpretation to choose. Record a clarification as a minor prompt.

## Distinguish passage questions from interface problems

When a participant is uncertain, let them finish their thought. Then ask:

- “Is the uncertainty coming from the passage or from something on the screen?”
- “What wording or control affected your understanding?”
- “What would help you find the evidence behind that statement?”

Record the participant's words separately from your interpretation. A
question about the sons of God, covenant, a textual witness, or a Greek term
may be productive passage engagement; do not label it a usability failure just
because it is unresolved. A failure to locate a source disclosure, an unclear
review label, or a blocked return path is an interface observation. If the
source of confusion remains unclear, mark it unclear rather than guessing.

## Record observations

For each task, note completion, assistance, approximate minutes, navigation
problems, confusing wording, source verification behavior, uncertainty or
review-label recognition, and whether a genuine follow-up question emerged.
Write observable behavior first (“opened the source disclosure after reading
the claim”) and facilitator interpretation separately (“the source label may
be unclear”). Do not infer comprehension from a nod, task completion, or
agreement with a claim.

Never describe a participant's interpretation as doctrinally wrong. Do not
correct, debate, or score it. The study evaluates the reader's usability and
evidence transparency, not theological agreement.

## Closing

Ask every post-session question in order, including the neutral question about
whether anything prompted further exploration. Accept “nothing,” “not sure,”
or no response without follow-up pressure. Thank the participant and explain
where the de-identified notes will be kept. Do not promise that BHF will adopt
every requested change.
