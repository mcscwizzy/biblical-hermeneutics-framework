# Measurement Rubric

This rubric scores observed interaction with the reader. It does not score
belief, biblical knowledge, doctrinal agreement, or whether a participant
adopts a scholarly proposal. Use **not observed** when evidence is missing;
never infer success from silence or task completion alone.

## Task-level record

For every assigned task, record one completion state:

| Field | Values | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Completion | completed / partially completed / not completed / not attempted | Mark the behavior observed, not the answer's theological content. |
| Assistance | none / minor prompt / substantial assistance / not observed | A neutral reminder is a minor prompt; pointing to the answer or doing the navigation is substantial. |
| Duration | approximate minutes or not observed | Start at the prompt and stop when the participant says they are done or moves on. |
| Verification | yes / partial / no / not applicable / not observed for each field | Rate source, claim link, locator, uncertainty, and review status separately. |
| Follow-up question | yes / no / unclear / not observed | “Yes” requires a question the participant actually expressed. |

Keep navigation problems, confusing wording, direct observations, facilitator
interpretations, and optional consented quotations in their separate fields.
For stopped or incomplete sessions, retain observations already made and leave
unobserved items explicit. Do not treat missing observations as failures.

## Five dimensions

Use these dimensions in the facilitator's interpretation after recording the
behavior. They summarize what was observed; they are not a single participant
score.

| Dimension | Evidence to observe | Positive evidence | Partial or blocked evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Participant opens relevant passage research without being told the control or record. | Finds research with no assistance or one neutral prompt. | Needs substantial direction, opens unrelated material, or cannot find research. |
| **Context** | Participant describes an original-text or audience observation and distinguishes it from a later scholarly interpretation. | Identifies the distinction in their own words, without needing to agree with either. | Notices one category but conflates them, or the distinction is not observed. |
| **Clarity** | Participant explains one observation or proposal in understandable language. | Gives a coherent paraphrase and identifies who proposes an interpretation when relevant. | Uses unclear wording, cannot explain the distinction, or not observed. |
| **Verification** | Participant connects a claim to a source and notices available locator, uncertainty, and review status. | Finds the source and claim relation; independently recognizes relevant caveats. | Finds only part of the trail, misses uncertainty/review labels, or needs substantial help. |
| **Continuity** | Participant follows a permitted connection or continues a question, then returns when requested. | Follows the verified direction and returns to the origin without assistance. | Needs a prompt, loses the origin, or route is unavailable. |

For each dimension, cite the task and direct observation that supports the
summary. If a task did not expose a dimension, report **not observed**. A
participant's disagreement with a claim is not a failure. A participant may
also notice a meaningful question without completing a navigation task.

## Denominators and reporting

Always show counts as `n / denominator` and label what the denominator means.
For task completion, the denominator is all sessions assigned that task;
assigned but unobserved tasks are reported as `not_observed`, unless the
facilitator recorded an explicit `not_attempted` task entry. For verification
fields, the denominator is recorded
observations with a value other than `not_observed`; state that rule. Duration
uses only recorded numeric task times. Do not compute a participant-level
success percentage across tasks or imply population estimates from 6–8
convenience participants.

The local analyzer reports aggregate counts and denominators and excludes raw
free-text notes, participant codes, and personal quotations. Qualitative
themes require a facilitator to review de-identified notes, group similar
observations, preserve counterexamples, and label interpretation as
interpretation. Do not publish quotations unless separate quotation consent
was explicitly recorded.
