# ED-5 clarity and terminology

The overview now contains a collapsed `details` disclosure with concise
definitions for the following CKL and reader terms:

| Term | Plain-language explanation |
|---|---|
| Textual witness | A surviving manuscript or ancient translation preserving a form of a passage. |
| Masoretic Text | A received Hebrew textual tradition transmitted with vocalization and Masoretic reading notes. |
| Septuagint | Ancient Greek translations of Jewish Scriptures, whose wording and history vary by book. |
| Critical apparatus | An edition's notes comparing manuscript and version readings. |
| Redaction | A proposal about how material was arranged or shaped during a text's history. |
| Reception history | How later readers or writers interpreted, quoted, adapted, or reused an earlier text. |
| Literary convention | A recurring form or writing practice; recognizing one does not prove authorial intent. |
| Parallelism | Related lines that repeat, develop, or contrast ideas; the exact structure can remain debated. |
| Lexical range | Meanings a word can carry across its uses; context helps assess which sense fits. |
| Historical reconstruction | A modern proposal about a past event or setting, limited by surviving evidence. |
| Ancient Near East | A broad label for ancient societies around the eastern Mediterranean and neighboring regions; similarity alone does not show borrowing. |
| Divine Council | A scholarly label for a heavenly assembly in some ancient texts; interpretations can differ. |
| Intertextual relationship | A proposed relationship from explicit quotation to possible echo; each depends on its evidence. |
| Disputed reading | Wording or meaning with competing proposals; the linked record retains sources, uncertainty, and review state. |

These definitions explain vocabulary. They do not resolve the selected
passages, replace the exact textual observation, or collapse the distinction
between observation and interpretation. Detailed evidence, source disclosure,
confidence, dispute status, and human-review notices remain in the existing
record presentation. Definitions are authored as reader vocabulary aids, not
as additions to CKL scholarship. Their wording follows existing BHF usage:

- **Ancient Near East** and **historical reconstruction** follow
  `framework/context/ancient-near-east.md`, which treats comparison as context,
  warns that parallels alone do not establish dependence, and labels
  reconstruction as uncertain.
- **Parallelism** and **lexical range** follow `framework/genres/poetry.md`,
  `framework/books/psalms.md`, and `framework/language/hebrew.md`; the latter
  says passage context narrows a word's attested range.
- **Intertextual relationship** follows
  `framework/core/04-intertextuality.md`, which distinguishes quotation,
  citation, echo, and thematic resemblance and requires confidence to track
  the strength of evidence.
- Textual-witness, Masoretic, Septuagint, and disputed-reading terms mirror
  the exact record labels and source identities in the selected manuscript
  records, including Psalm 145:13 and Deuteronomy 32:8–9. The guide does not
  choose an original reading.
- **Divine Council** follows the domain and uncertainty labels in
  `framework/canonical_library/objects/cultural_background/ancient-divine-assembly-imagery.json`;
  the guide does not assert a consensus interpretation.

The guide's effect on actual reader comprehension requires future usability
sessions.
