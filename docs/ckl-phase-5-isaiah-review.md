## Human review checklist

Verify:

- every superscription, named prophet reference, first-person voice,
  third-person report, divine speech, herald, servant, communal speaker, city
  personification, and unresolved speaker;
- the six practical units, alternative macrostructures, seams, inclusios,
  catchwords, recursions, bridges, and proposed editorial stages;
- Isaiah of Jerusalem's setting and every proposed eighth-, seventh-, sixth-,
  fifth-, or later-century layer;
- the Syro-Ephraimite crisis, Assyrian campaigns, Samaria's fall, 701 BCE,
  Babylonian exile, Cyrus's conquest, Persian return, and postexilic
  community reconstructions;
- authorship, discipleship, collection, redaction, final-form, predictive, and
  confessional positions at their stated certainty levels;
- every genre assignment, especially sign report, servant poem, Isaiah
  24–27, city poetry, trial speech, and new-creation language;
- *qadosh yisrael*, sign names, *almah*, royal titles, *netser*, *heylel
  ben-shachar*, *eved*, *mashiach*, herald forms, *go'el*, *ruach*, *tsom*,
  *shabbat*, covenant expressions, and Greek equivalents;
- Immanuel's immediate identity, time horizon, Isaiah 8 reuse, Hebrew
  *almah*, Greek *parthenos*, and Matthew 1;
- Isaiah 9 and 11's historical, royal, ideal, messianic, and christological
  readings;
- Isaiah 14's human royal addressee, mythic imagery, Septuagint and Vulgate
  wording, and later Lucifer-Satan reception;
- every servant occurrence, shifts between singular and plural, collective
  and individual readings, and the unnamed servant in 52:13–53:12;
- Jewish servant and Isaiah 53 reception without Christian myths of
  suppression or recent invention;
- Cyrus as shepherd and anointed, Persian ideology, the Cyrus Cylinder as
  comparison, and limits on political analogy;
- Isaiah 6 hardening in Isaiah, the Gospels, Acts, and Romans without
  antisemitism or supersessionism;
- blindness, deafness, lameness, sickness, cognition, and healing language
  with disability-aware interpretation;
- daughter-city, marriage, stripping, bereavement, infertility, labor, birth,
  and mother imagery with feminist, womanist, and trauma-aware safeguards;
- empire, warfare, divine agency, collective punishment, forced migration,
  displacement, refugee trauma, return, and nation language;
- worship, sacrifice, fasting, food, housing, oppression, repair, Sabbath,
  foreigners, eunuchs, and prosperity misuse;
- Isaiah 25–27's death and resurrection language and Isaiah 65–66's
  continuity, transformation, judgment, and later eschatological reception;
- every Masoretic, 1QIsa-a, 1QIsa-b, other Qumran, pesher, Old Greek, and New
  Testament textual claim passage by passage;
- every use of prediction, historical referent, typology, quotation,
  allusion, canonical synthesis, Jewish interpretation, christology, and
  ecclesiology;
- every source locator, support target, certainty label, dispute label,
  relationship, and pastoral application.

Do not advance the record merely because automated checks pass. Advance only
the sections actually reviewed, and record reviewer identity and date.
