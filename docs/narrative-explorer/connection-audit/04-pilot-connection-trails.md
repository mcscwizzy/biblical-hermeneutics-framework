# Pilot connection trails

## Reading the trails

“Navigable” below means that CKL records an authored relationship with a resolvable destination and the current passage-scoped projection can expose the source record. A passage anchor means only that a record applies to a passage. The current interface distinguishes Scripture/comparative anchors from relationship edges. Shared topic retrieval, broad book-level `related_objects`, or an anchor at both ends is not counted as a cross-passage relationship.

## Genesis 1–3: creation, Eden, sacred space, expulsion

### Genesis 1 — creation and ancient comparison

- **Existing and navigable:** The `creation` event has authored links to `creation-theme` (`thematic-anchor`), `creation-doctrine` (`doctrinal-anchor`), `creation-doctrine-framework` (`related`), `the-fall` (`narrative-contrast`), `new-creation-theme` (`eschatological-fulfillment`), and `mesopotamian-creation-and-flood-comparisons` (`comparative-ancient-text-context`). The Creation/New Creation route is a topic-level route, not a passage-specific quotation claim. `mesopotamian-creation-and-flood-comparisons#evidence:genesis-ordered-worldview-observation` and `#evidence:enuma-elish-cosmic-ordering-comparison` have reciprocal `compared-with` links and source records `genesis-1-11-text`, `sarna-genesis-comparison`, and `lambert-babylonian-creation-2013`.
- **Existing but not fully surfaced:** The two comparison evidence records are separate child records. Their authored evidence-to-evidence edge exists, but the current detail navigator accepts canonical object destinations and does not open a sibling `#evidence:` destination as a supported trail target. The comparison remains visible as evidence; its linked evidence destination is the A-class navigation gap.
- **Evidence-rich records without a further documented bridge:** `what-does-ruach-mean`, `what-is-the-holy-spirit`, `what-is-the-image-of-god`, and `what-is-the-significance-of-the-holy-spirit` can be projected from their applicable records, but a shared anchor alone does not provide an authored edge from those questions to a creation record. Some lack a reader section assignment. This is a placement/discoverability issue, not evidence of a missing biblical relationship.
- **Candidate:** John 1’s scoped Logos claim provides a separate candidate bridge to Genesis 1; see the John 1 section. No new Genesis 1–ANE edge is needed for the comparison pair already linked in CKL.
- **Do not promote:** Do not turn the Enuma comparison into direct borrowing or a settled chronology. Its comparison claim records major disagreement, and its comparative temporal marker does not establish dependence.

### Genesis 2 — garden, rivers, sacred space

- **Existing and navigable:** `genesis#evidence:g2-garden-planting-presence` is anchored to Genesis 2:8–10 and 3:8, cites `genesis-1-50`, `sarna-genesis`, and `g1-amit-eden-exposition`, and links to `creation` and `the-fall` as `narrative-context`. NE-8B’s pilot confirms the garden record surfaces with its scoped evidence. `g2-rivers-and-eden-geographic-limits` is anchored to Genesis 2:10–14, cites the Genesis text and Sarna, and links to `euphrates` as a named river. Its authored qualification says the other named rivers are unidentified and the text does not locate Eden.
- **Existing but not surfaced as an edge:** The garden and river records’ common Genesis 2 scope does not create an edge between them. `what-does-ruach-mean` and `what-is-the-significance-of-the-sabbath` are also projected without a reader-section assignment in this scope.
- **Evidence-rich records without a meaningful onward trail:** `g2-toledot-and-genesis1-relationship`, `g2-work-and-keep-vocation`, and `g2-two-trees-and-knowing-good-evil` have passage-scoped sources and interpretations. The `work-and-keep` item explicitly treats priestly-service parallels as later interpretation, not as a stated priestly office in Genesis.
- **Candidate:** None is required to make the garden/rivers evidence discoverable. Any link from the river evidence to `euphrates` should stay a named-river association and not be broadened into an Eden location claim.
- **Do not promote:** Do not infer Eden coordinates, an Eden site identity, or physical sacred geography from the Euphrates association or the sacred-space interpretation.

### Genesis 3 — expulsion, cherubim, later reception

- **Existing and navigable:** `the-fall` links to `creation` (`narrative-contrast`), `exile-theme` (`thematic-development`), `resurrection-theme` (`redemptive-answer`), and `covenant-theme` (`theological-context`). `g3-eastward-expulsion-and-flaming-weapon` links to `the-fall` and `exile-theme`; `g3-cherubim-biblical-sacred-space-context` links to `the-fall`. The latter cites `genesis-1-50`, `g1-exodus-text`, `g1-kings-text`, and `g1-lexham-cherubim` while distinguishing Genesis’s scene from later sanctuary references.
- **Later reception present but not directly linked onward:** `g3-serpent-later-canonical-reception` and `g3-seed-later-jewish-christian-reception` currently link to `the-fall`. The serpent record discusses Revelation 12:9 as later canonical identification, but it has no authored object edge to Revelation. Its source IDs include `g1-revelation-text` and `g1-zhakevich-genesis-315`.
- **Supported candidates:** A separately reviewed later-canonical-reception edge from `g3-serpent-later-canonical-reception` to `revelation` could expose the cited later identification while keeping it distinct from Genesis’s wording. A separate sacred-space comparison from the cherubim evidence toward tabernacle/temple records could be reviewed from `g1-exodus-text`, `g1-kings-text`, and `g1-lexham-cherubim`.
- **Do not promote:** Do not make the later serpent identification the plain meaning of Genesis 3:15. Do not equate Eden with the tabernacle or temple; the comparison records do not establish that identity. Do not make national exile and Eden’s expulsion identical because `the-fall` links to `exile-theme`.

## Genesis 6: sons of God, Nephilim, flood, later reception

- **Existing and navigable:** `genesis-flood-postflood-context` links to `the-flood`, `noah`, `creation`, `the-fall`, `covenant-theme`, `image-of-god-theme`, and `mesopotamian-creation-and-flood-comparisons`. Evidence records `g6-sons-of-god-interpretive-positions`, `g6-nephilim-and-mighty-men`, and `g6-one-hundred-twenty-years` each have their own scoped connection to `the-flood`. The NE-8B preview shows disputed sons-of-God interpretations, later Watchers reception, and a Gilgamesh comparison as separate cards.
- **Existing but incompletely navigable:** The three records have authored onward paths, but there is no evidence-level bridge among the sons-of-God positions, Nephilim wording, and Watchers reception. `g6-second-temple-watchers-reception` cites `g2-1-enoch-6`, `g2-jubilees-5`, and `g2-doedens-sons-god`. Its Genesis anchor is currently marked `direct` / `contemporary` even though the record itself labels the material as later reception. Preserve this as a temporal-metadata concern for source-owner review; do not silently correct or reinterpret it.
- **Later New Testament interpretation:** A 1 Peter interpretive note is anchored to 1 Peter 3:19–20 and Genesis 6:1–22, with sources `1pet-greek`, `dss`, and `michaels`; it preserves multiple readings of the spirits in prison. Jude’s object has a Genesis 6:1–4 background anchor, but the Jude claim `jude-angels` is anchored to Jude 1:6 and says the Watchers association is debated. Jude 1:14–15’s quotation of 1 Enoch 1:9 is a distinct record from the Watchers material in 1 Enoch 6–16.
- **Supported candidate:** A carefully bounded interpretive-association route from the Watchers reception evidence to Jude could be reviewed against the Genesis evidence sources above and Jude’s `enoch`, `davids`, and `levine-jant` sources. The edge must say later reception / disputed association, not direct quotation or confirmation of the Genesis interpretation.
- **Evidence-rich records without a new bridge:** The Nephilim item’s sole onward edge is to the Flood and explicitly says Genesis 6:4 does not unambiguously identify the Nephilim with the offspring or the mighty men. Numbers 13:33 is mentioned in notes as later comparison, not as a physical description or passage anchor.
- **Do not promote:** Do not merge Genesis 6, 1 Enoch’s Watchers, Jude’s angels, and Jude’s Enoch quotation into one event or one settled interpretation. Do not create a sons-of-God-to-Nephilim identity edge from adjacent verses alone.

## Deuteronomy 32:8–9 — textual witnesses and nations

- **Existing and navigable:** `ancient-divine-assembly-imagery` links to `deuteronomy` as `textual-variant-context`, with the note that Deuteronomy 32:8–9 requires manuscript-specific handling. Its Deuteronomy claim `deuteronomy-32-8-textual-witnesses` is marked disputed / textual variant; evidence `deuteronomy-32-8-manuscript-variant` cites `deuteronomy-32-text` and `ulrich-biblical-qumran-scrolls-2010`. It distinguishes witness date from disputed poem-composition horizons and cautions against deriving an entire worldview from one variant.
- **Passage scope versus cross-passage link:** The assembly object’s anchor at Deuteronomy 32:8–9 applies the record. The object’s separate Psalm 82 anchor does not establish a direct Deuteronomy-to-Psalm relationship. The reverse book edge has a different label, so it is not an exact reciprocal pair.
- **Evidence-rich but without a direct edge:** Justice objects `justice-theme`, `righteousness-theme`, `divine-justice`, `mishpat`, and `tsedeq` are separately anchored to Deuteronomy 32:3–4, while assembly/textual-variant material is anchored to 32:8–9. No object edge links these networks; co-retrieval does not create one.
- **Do not promote:** Do not present a disputed textual variant as an uncontested divine-council conclusion. Do not attach the Psalm 82 Ugaritic comparison to Deuteronomy 32 merely because both records are under the same assembly object.

## Psalm 82:1–8 — assembly language, justice, comparison

- **Existing and navigable:** Claim `psalm-82-assembly-text` is textually explicit about the assembly and plural address, while marking the figures’ identity as disputed. Evidence `psalm-82-assembly-language` is medium-confidence worldview evidence with interpretive uncertainty; it lists heavenly beings, human rulers/judges, nations’ patrons, and rhetorically demoted powers as readings. The object links to `psalms` as book context.
- **Ancient comparison kept distinct:** The separate Ugaritic claim/evidence (`ugaritic-divine-assembly-comparison`, `ugaritic-council-comparison`) cites `smith-ugaritic-baal-cycle-1994`, `smith-origins-biblical-monotheism-2001`, and `lewis-origin-character-god-2020`. It has earlier-comparative scope at Psalm 82 and Psalm 89 and explicitly does not settle continuity or Psalm 82’s referents. Its evidence-to-evidence reverse label is different (`northwest-semitic-comparison` / `compared-with`), so both edges are one-way under exact-label counting.
- **Possible candidate:** The Psalm evidence’s explicit judicial language could support review of a narrowly scoped evidence-to-`justice-theme` or `divine-justice` edge, using `psalm-82-hebrew-text`. It is absent now; the overlap should not be inferred from co-retrieved Deuteronomy justice records.
- **Do not promote:** Do not project the Ugaritic evidence onto Deuteronomy 32. Its own anchors are Psalm 82 and Psalm 89, not Deuteronomy. Do not turn comparative material into proof of borrowing or an agreed ontology.

## Exodus 3:1–15 — burning bush, presence, holy ground, Horeb/Sinai

- **Existing and navigable:** `burning-bush` is anchored to Exodus 3:1–15; `mount-sinai` to 3:1–6; `divine-presence` and `presence-theme` to 3:1–6; and `exodus-to-sinai-timeline` to 3:1–15. Existing edges include burning bush to `sinai-covenant` and `moses`, Mount Sinai to Moses and Sinai covenant, and timeline to Sinai covenant. These are scoped records, not all edges between co-anchored records.
- **Missing bridge with existing material:** No authored edge joins `burning-bush` to `mount-sinai`, though the place record describes the burning bush at Horeb and both records apply to Exodus 3. A location/context edge may be considered after source-owner review, with Horeb/Sinai naming and geographical uncertainty preserved.
- **Research gap:** No passage-specific claim/evidence item for the holy-ground wording at Exodus 3:5 was found. The existing presence concepts are theme/theology records; their anchors do not supply a dedicated evidence argument. The NE-5A sacred-space review explicitly keeps presence concepts in Themes and does not turn the physical mountain into cosmic geography.
- **Do not promote:** Do not infer an exact modern mountain identification, a settled Horeb/Sinai geography, or a sacred-space type from common passage anchors. A `golden-calf` record’s Exodus 3 anchor is a semantic misanchor, not a valid Exodus 3 trail.

## John 1:1–18 — creation language, Word, divine presence

- **Existing and navigable:** `ckl:john#claim:john-logos` has a John 1:1–18 scope and a Genesis 1:1–5 scope, with sources `john-greek`, `boyarin-logos`, and `oxford-johannine`. `creation`, `creation-theme`, and `new-creation-theme` provide a topic-level route toward the New Creation material. Presence/sanctuary themes are separately anchored at John 1:14 and Revelation 21:1–3.
- **Existing but not surfaced as a cross-passage edge:** The two claim anchors establish the claim’s applicability; they do not produce a Genesis 1 ↔ John 1 relationship. The current passage projection shows only the anchors relevant to its current passage. There is no direct John-to-Revelation edge among the shared presence-theme records.
- **Supported candidate:** A separately reviewed `proposed_intertextual_echo` edge between John 1:1–5 and Genesis 1:1–5 could expose the sourced comparison. The claim’s literary genealogy is debated; do not label it a quotation, direct dependence, or settled theological equivalence.
- **Do not promote:** Do not convert the Presence Theme’s John/Revelation anchors or shared `new-temple-theme` association into a claim that John 1 and Revelation 21 are literary equivalents.

## Revelation 21:1–22:5 — new creation, dwelling, garden imagery

- **Existing and navigable:** The existing Creation/New Creation seed links `creation` and `creation-theme` to `new-creation-theme`; John 1:1–5 and Revelation 21:1–5 are anchors on that theme route. `new-jerusalem` is anchored to Revelation 21:1–27 / 22:1–5 and also to Isaiah 65:17–25, Isaiah 66:22–23, and Ezekiel 48:30–35. `presence-theme`, `divine-presence`, `sanctuary-theme`, and `new-temple-theme` have separate John 1:14 and/or Revelation 21:1–3 anchors. These are navigable topic paths, not a direct passage-to-passage edge.
- **Claim-level candidates with material already in CKL:** `revelation#claim:rev-new-jerusalem` is scoped to Revelation 21:9–22:5 and cites `rev-greek`, `bauckham`, and `moyise`; the `ezekiel#claim:ezekiel-revelation-reuse` is scoped to Revelation 21:1–22:5 and cites `new-testament-ezekiel`, `mein-gog-politics`, and `oxford-handbook-ezekiel`. The CKL describes city/bride/sanctuary/Eden and Ezekielian city/river/tree imagery. Candidate passage-scoped edges to Genesis 2–3 and Ezekiel 40–48 need separate scope and source review; broad Revelation-book anchors alone are not enough.
- **Other authored but untyped lead:** A Zechariah object reference marks Revelation 22:1–5 as `parallel`; `zechariah#claim:zechariah-living-water-kingship` is scoped to Zechariah 14:8–11 and cites `zechariah-masoretic`, `boda-zechariah`, and `foster-theology`. The anchor is useful scope metadata, but is not itself a traversable relationship. A shared-motif edge could be considered after review; it should not be called a quotation.
- **Evidence-rich records without passage-specific onward trails:** The Isaiah book object contains free-text `new_testament_connections` / cross-reference material, and the `new-jerusalem` event carries Isaiah anchors. Neither field creates a source-backed Revelation edge on its own. Revelation’s broad `rev-scriptural-reuse` scope covers the whole book and cannot establish a specific source passage.
- **Do not promote:** Do not infer that Eden, Zion, Ezekiel’s temple/city, and Revelation’s New Jerusalem are identical places or one continuous historical event. Keep the distinct canonical and interpretive layers visible; do not treat `canonical-fulfillment` or `presence-fulfillment` as self-validating types.

## Cross-pilot result

The CKL already contains many usable passage anchors, evidence records, and thematic routes. The highest-value additions are selective navigation/classification repairs and a small number of scoped edge candidates—not a blanket Scripture-anchor conversion. Ancient comparisons (for example, Ugaritic and Mesopotamian material) and later reception (Watchers, Jude, and Revelation) must remain separate from claims about the wording or historical setting of the earlier passage.
