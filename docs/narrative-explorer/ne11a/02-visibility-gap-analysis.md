# NE-11A evidence-to-reader visibility analysis

## Current path from CKL to reader

| Stage | Current rule | Consequence for discovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CKL retrieval | Narrative Explorer performs a full-inventory lookup by the selected Scripture reference and reports complete, partial, or failed status. Study Companion builds its own passage context and resource status. | A complete empty response means “not represented in this pathway,” not “no relation exists.” A failed/partial response remains unknown or visibly incomplete. |
| Passage eligibility | An authored route needs a current CKL related-object/evidence edge, a valid NE-9C curation, an applicable passage scope that overlaps the selected passage, and a distinct, valid one-chapter destination. A bridge needs an exact NE-10 registry record whose endpoints are supported by its exact claim/evidence/note, whose source IDs are attached to that record and resolve in the parent object. | Shared anchors, topics, parent objects, vocabulary, or bibliography do not create a route. Book-level relationship edges without a scoped curation do not qualify. |
| Curation and provenance safeguards | NE-9C curated decisions authorize authored edges; held and excluded decisions do not. NE-10 records carry relationship type, direction/layer, exact support identities, attached sources, explanation, and uncertainty. Every supporting bridge record must support both endpoints. | A route can be absent even when a CKL parent mentions both books. That protects readers from implying quotation, borrowing, identity, or consensus that the record does not establish. |
| Projection | The projection returns authored relationships and verified bridges in separate lists, with exact destinations and support. It reports `available`, `not_represented`, `partial`, or `unknown`; failed lookups cannot claim complete coverage. | NE-10 route records are not merged into authored CKL graph edges. Reciprocal endpoint views of one bridge are one bridge record. |
| Narrative Explorer display | The Related Biblical Passages section displays returned destinations and supports passage navigation. Evidence previews use a separate selector: evidence records only, with a source identity, passage anchor, and a supported related edge with basis notes; up to three are selected using focus/geography and evidence-kind ranking. | Claim-backed routes can be visible in the route list while their supporting claim is not an “Evidence about this passage” preview. This is a selector contract, not proof the claim was omitted from retrieval. |
| Study Companion display | The current deterministic local presentation shows at most three discovery cards and one land card. A land card duplicates no map place already visible. Card actions route `related_passages` to generic cross-references, not to a particular verified NE-10 destination. | A visible card may have evidence but no precise passage route action. This is the clearest low-risk engineering gap found. |

## Exact result states

1. **Returned and displayed:** an eligible authored destination or verified bridge appears in Related Biblical Passages; visible cards/previews are separately counted. A complete projection with routes is `available`.
2. **Returned but not displayed as a preview/card:** a claim-backed route may be displayed in the route section, while the preview selector rejects its claim because it accepts evidence records only. Presentation card limits can also leave a valid card out of view. A hidden card is not evidence that its CKL record was not retrieved.
3. **Eligible but not retrieved:** the sample census found no complete-query failure or confirmed missing eligible authored route. Where an exact route exists only in a record not connected to a valid curated edge or NE-10 bridge, it is not eligible for the passage route list. The current selector intentionally does not retrieve claims as preview cards.
4. **Held:** the seven NE-10 research candidates remain outside confirmed bridge routes. Their reasons and exact identities are detailed below and in the prior NE-10 audit.
5. **Unsupported as a passage relationship:** shared theme, a citation, or multiple book references alone; a broad metadata field without an exact child record; or a record that does not establish both scopes. Such material may remain useful context.
6. **Unavailable/incomplete:** failed or partial CKL retrieval; a bridge support object/record/source missing; or an invalid anchor. The 16 sample builds were complete. The one invalid Obadiah note and two unresolved source IDs are listed in the baseline report.

- **D — missing specific route research:** `jonah#claim:jonah-three-days`, citing Jonah 1:17 and Matthew 12:40 with `jonah-masoretic`, `jonah-new-testament`, and `graybill-kaltner-mckenzie-jonah`; no NE-9C authored relationship identity or NE-10 bridge. - **C — held for evidence/scholarly review:** exact NE-10 candidates and scopes are `deuteronomy-32-psalm-82-job-1-divine-assembly` (Deuteronomy 32:8–9 / Psalm 82:1–8 / Job 1:6–12), `isaiah-43-exodus-12-15-new-exodus` (Isaiah 43:14–21 / Exodus 12–15), `ezekiel-40-48-revelation-21-22-temple` (Ezekiel 40–48 / Revelation 21–22), `ezekiel-37-john-3-water-spirit` (Ezekiel 37 / John 3:5–8 / John 7:37–39), `exodus-12-1-corinthians-5-passover` (Exodus 12:21–30 / 1 Corinthians 5:7–8), `psalm-110-acts-2-enthronement` (Psalm 110:1 / Acts 2:22–36), and `genesis-2-3-revelation-21-22-eden-tree-life` (Genesis 2–3 / Revelation 21–22). The held registry assigns none a relationship category or candidate-level source IDs; where a relevant CKL object or child claim exists, its exact identity and source set are described in the ranked report. - **F — technical/data review:** the Obadiah 7 invalid anchor and the unanchored Roman Ephesus note’s two unresolved source IDs. Neither establishes an eligible passage relationship; resolve data ownership before drawing a coverage conclusion.

Negative examples that should stay out of the confirmed route list: (1) Genesis 1 / Enuma Elish is comparative context, not direct dependence; (2) Deuteronomy 32 / Psalm 82 / Job 1 cannot be inferred from a common parent, divine-assembly topic, or source; (3) Genesis 2–3 does not establish Eden’s modern location; (4) Genesis 6 does not settle the identity of the sons of God and Nephilim; and (5) Isaiah 43’s new-exodus wording does not identify an exact Exodus passage in the supporting child claim. In each case, further research may be warranted, but a route should not be created from the current metadata alone.
