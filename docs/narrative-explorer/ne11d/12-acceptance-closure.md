## Journey timing samples

The timing tests reload the app and reset selection before each positive journey in one browser and server session per passage. First versus later samples are cold-ish and warm within that session, not controlled cold-cache measurements; other test processes ran concurrently. Numbers are milliseconds, from browser resource timing and monotonic action-to-focus and destination-selection intervals. Resource entries capture completed requests visible at collection, not every request that was in flight or cancelled. Zero `transferSize` for Companion responses is not evidence of zero payload.

The repeated Genesis source projection was 236,489 encoded bytes and the John destination projection 196,673; the Genesis and John Companion bodies were 142,885 and 113,277 encoded bytes. Each Genesis sample recorded five progressively scoped selection requests, four with zero encoded bytes, in addition to chapter and destination Companion entries. The repeated Jonah source projection was 141,151 encoded bytes (141,451 transfer bytes), and the Matthew projection 79,826 encoded bytes (80,126 transfer bytes). Jonah and Matthew Companion bodies showed 134,189 and 167,523 encoded bytes respectively; their reported transfer sizes were zero. Each sample had two requests for the identical Jonah projection: preflight and required activation verification. One chapter Companion request had zero encoded bytes and may have been cancelled; the resource timing entries alone do not establish cancellation or total final request count. Earlier failed timing setup was corrected by resetting the passage before each sample; it supplied no completed three-sample evidence.

The real-data negative test made an explicit projection request after selecting each passage. In one run, Psalm 22:1 returned `not_represented` for related passages, no action, 9 projection items, a 113,678-byte encoded projection in 1,276 ms, and a 116,653-byte selected-passage Companion body. Psalm 119:176 likewise had no routes or actions but had 7 projection items; its 52,443-byte encoded projection took 2,766 ms and its Companion body was 114,731 bytes. Each had two Companion and one projection resource entries at collection; the manually requested projection is not an automatic preflight measurement. Neither is a no-evidence projection. No activation or destination timing applies when no action exists.

The bounded real API scan covered Leviticus 27:34, Numbers 33:56, Ezekiel 48:35, Jeremiah 52:34, Job 41:34, Psalms 150:6, Romans 16:27 and Acts 28:31; their item counts were 2, 2, 6, 9, 7, 3, 31 and 21. This does not prove the absence of a zero-evidence passage.

For a narrower matched technical comparison, `test_ne11d3_projection_baseline.py` made three identical Genesis 1:1–5 HTTP requests in each isolated temporary server, using the same Python environment and current harness. The NE-11C1 tree was extracted from commit `628f7d13e35d77297586751f584c5a32c06d570d` to a separate temporary directory. Current response times were 10,661, 1,433 and 1,428 ms; NE-11C1 times were 31,243, 1,514 and 1,495 ms. Each body was 236,489 bytes. These local runs overlapped other work, did not clear all caches, and are not a controlled latency comparison. An attempt to execute the same Genesis Companion handoff against NE-11C1 timed out waiting for the action; therefore no equivalent baseline action-to-focus or destination timing is claimed.
