## Research and uncertainty

The wave focused on historical people, places, and events, with a smaller group of kingship and kingdom relationships. Curated links use passage anchors owned by the exact source object and cite resolvable CKL source records. The claims include narrative settings at Athens, Capernaum, Bethany, Hebron, and Kiriath-arba; Paul’s Corinthian activity and Galatian assemblies; and Lydia’s household. The observations distinguish what the biblical narrative states from unsettled reconstruction: Athens is a literary speech rather than a recoverable transcript, Galatian city identification remains debated, Lydia’s household membership and status are unspecified, and a Gospel setting does not identify an excavated building.

The held Fall of Jerusalem–Babylonian Chronicles edge preserves the historical distinction between Nebuchadnezzar’s 597 BCE capture and the later destruction narrated in 2 Kings 25. BM 21946 documents the earlier campaign and cannot establish the proposed later-event link. The Abraham–Shechem direction was also held where Abraham’s object lacks Genesis 12:6–7 as an owned passage; the independently authored reverse-direction edge was assessed on its own source scope. These decisions keep textual evidence, historical correlation, and authored direction distinct.
