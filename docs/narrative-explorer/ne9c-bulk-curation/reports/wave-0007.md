## Research and curation

The selected passages were checked against the root Scripture anchors on both exact endpoint records. Aggregated child-claim context was not treated as direct applicability. Curated entries retain the source-authored direction and metadata; reciprocal Pilate edges were reviewed separately.

The synagogue and civic-context connections use Luke 4:16–21, Acts 13:14–16, Acts 17:1–9, and Acts 16:25–34 for the specific narrated settings. They do not generalize a single synagogue practice or household structure to all cities. The day-of-the-Lord connection to Revelation 6:12–17 is thematic and does not fix a timetable or referent.

Some Pilate-context edges remain as bounded associations where their attached evidence supports the stated claim. The Pilate Stone-to-Roman Governorship edge was held during acceptance because its cited biblical passage and generic museum record did not substantiate the inscription’s prefectural-title claim with usable epigraphic evidence. The inscription is not treated as evidence for the Gospel trial.

John 14:15–26 anchors the Parakletos and Spirit links; the Greek vocabulary is kept in view rather than reduced to one English gloss. The Torah-to-Galatians comparison is explicitly cross-language: Galatians 3:23–25 has Greek *nomos*, not Hebrew *Torah*. John 14:27 uses Greek *eirēnē*, so its relation to *shalom* is thematic rather than a Hebrew word occurrence. Psalm 103:8–13 grounds the mercy/*hesed* pair, with uncertainty about the Hebrew term’s range retained.

The new covenant prophecy connects directly to Jeremiah 31:31–34; the Hebrews connection is later reception, and Hebrews 8:8–12 quotes Jeremiah’s oracle. Abraham is treated as later reception in Hebrews 11 and Romans 4. Exodus 19, Numbers 10, Exodus 29, and Judges 21 support their specific narrative settings. Comparative portable-sanctuary material is used as context only: formal parallels do not demonstrate direct borrowing. [Homan, *To Your Tents, O Israel* (open-access edition)](https://www.sidestone.com/openaccess/9789088904967.pdf).

The Temple Warning Inscription connection to Acts 21:27–36 is contextual. The inscription attests a restricted boundary and its warning; it does not verify every element of Luke’s account. Hebrews 9 links are labeled later canonical reception and explicitly distinguish the letter’s “greater tent” theology from a historical reconstruction of the Jerusalem temple. The new-creation connection uses Revelation 21’s symbolic city vision and is not presented as a simple prediction about the historical city.
