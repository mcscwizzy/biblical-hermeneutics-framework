## Research and uncertainty

The reviewed material covers Pauline community ethics and networks, covenant and new-covenant language, exile and Persian-period history, Genesis geography and genealogies, creation and flood motifs, temple restoration, prophetic reception, and the Ephesians and Thessalonian letters. Each curated entry preserves the authored direction and metadata, uses a passage from the source object’s own Scripture anchors, and cites resolvable CKL source records. The additional text-critical research for Ephesians 1:1 represents competing explanations of the omission and inclusion of “in Ephesus” in early witnesses, following David A. deSilva’s [Cambridge University Press Ephesians excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/93710/excerpt/9781108493710_excerpt.pdf). The Thessalonica-to-2 Thessalonians entry retains the open authorship debate documented in [New Testament Studies](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/who-wrote-ii-thessalonians/B01799225644E98E742A16898F98E377) and Paul Foster’s [Journal for the Study of the New Testament article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0142064x12462654).

Uncertainty includes manuscript readings and disputed authorship, competing interpretations of Pauline language and social roles, distinctions between original setting and later reception, limits of ancient royal and archaeological evidence, disputed place identifications, and the difference between shared motifs and demonstrable literary dependence. For example, the Salem-to-Jerusalem proposal remains held because Genesis 14 names Salem without identifying it as Jerusalem; J. A. Emerton’s study of the location is linked in that decision record.
