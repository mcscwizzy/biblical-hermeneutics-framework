## Research and uncertainty

This wave was weighted toward kingship, messianic expectation, and kingdom (240 entries), alongside historical, lexical, cultural, and other canonical-context relationships. Curated cases use source-owned passages and preserve the difference between direct textual evidence and later interpretation. Examples include Galatians 3's reception of Abrahamic promise; Isaiah 53's disputed servant/sacrifice and messianic interpretation; Romans 8's adoption and inheritance language; Hebrews 1's scriptural citations; and Revelation's symbolic kingdom imagery. The New Jerusalem is not labeled “kingdom” by Revelation 21 itself. Mark 7:19 retains its syntactic ambiguity and is not generalized into a claim that all food laws are abolished.

Historical and cultural claims were bounded by their evidence. BM 21946 supports Nebuchadnezzar's 597 BCE campaign and is not evidence for the later 586 BCE destruction. The Merneptah inscription supplies an Egyptian attestation of a people called Israel, not verification of Exodus or a settled account of Israelite origins or territory. Roman readings of Revelation and Philippians remain interpretive and debated. For Philippi, research included the Acts 16:12 civic designation and the debated genre and imperial resonance of Philippians 2; those sources did not justify the broad authored cluster link, which remains held. Other consulted scholarship included work on Phoebe's `diakonos`/`prostatis` terminology, Mark 7 Greek syntax, Thessalonian civic/funerary context, and Hebrew `ruach` and `hesed` usage. Competing interpretations are recorded in the decision notes and citations rather than collapsed into consensus.
