## Research and uncertainty

Review prioritized narrative, canonical, historical, and literary relationships across covenant and restoration, people and events, kingship, temple, prophetic reception, and Second Temple context. Each curated decision retains its exact authored identity and direction, scoped passages, CKL source records, Scripture support, explanation, textual observation, and uncertainty. The record distinguishes explicit textual naming from later reception, interpretive association, and shared motif; shared vocabulary alone was not treated as proof of literary dependence.

Recurring uncertainty includes disputed chronology and geography, the limits of archaeological or royal-inscription evidence, differences between biblical and external source genres, semantic range and translation, literary formation, and competing Jewish and Christian receptions. Held decisions preserve source-scope and passage-applicability limits. Excluded decisions identify generic or insufficiently useful links. A focused projection regression changed 22 initially curated decisions to held where the exact authored direction could not be projected at a supported passage or was suppressed by the existing book-level safeguard. Three entity-to-theme/event entries were narrowed to passages cited by both endpoint records and reclassified as thematic or interpretive associations; they are not direct textual references between Bible texts. No safeguard or API behavior was weakened.
