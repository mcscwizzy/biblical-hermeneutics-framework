## Research and uncertainty

Direct textual links were separated from later interpretation and thematic comparison. For example, the decision on Acts 15 and 4QMMT remains held: the texts illuminate distinct Jewish legal discussions, but the available evidence does not make them the same dispute. The 597 BCE Babylonian campaign represented by BM 21946 remains distinct from Jerusalem’s later destruction in 586 BCE. Neither chronology nor historical setting is collapsed into a generalized “exile” link.

Temple and priesthood entries use the passages that are actually anchored by both endpoints. During regression, the Priesthood access link was corrected from Exodus 28:1–4, which was only anchored by the target object, to Leviticus 16:1–34, which is anchored by both objects and directly treats restricted sanctuary entry. Its CKL provenance now points to the source records `leviticus-access-text` and `leviticus-8-16`. This correction preserves the authored edge identity and does not change any CKL source record.

Other retained uncertainties include the debated civic resonance of *parousia* language, Roman-imperial interpretations of Thessalonian texts, the scope of prophetic reuse, the semantic range of Hebrew *ruaḥ* and *ḥesed*, and the relation between sanctuary descriptions and ancient portable shrines. Comparison is not treated as proof of literary dependence. Junia’s phrase in Romans 16:7 and Phoebe’s titles in Romans 16:1–2 retain competing readings and the distinction between the Greek wording and later institutional categories. The specific readings and bibliographic provenance are preserved in the wave data and decision records.

Primary and scholarly material consulted includes the [4QMMT transcription](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/courses/427/texts/4QMMT.htm), an [Oxford treatment of purity](https://academic.oup.com/book/62367/chapter/554648603), the [British Museum record for the Temple Warning inscription](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_C-328), research on the [Temple Warning as a Greek visitor text](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/reading-the-temple-warning-as-a-greek-visitor/), and studies of [Phoebe’s terminology](https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/7983), [Junia’s identity and description](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/a-reexamination-of-romans-167-in-light-of-primary-source-materials/299EA8DE87721790DB9388E42841F0E6), and [prophecy and gender in Paul and Plutarch](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/paul-plutarch-and-the-gender-dynamics-of-prophecy/010C5933799D0757AAD949AB8497E3AF). These publications inform bounded readings; they do not establish a consensus where the decision records preserve disagreement.
