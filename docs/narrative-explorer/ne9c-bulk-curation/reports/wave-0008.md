## Research and curation

Wave 8 included canonical-theology concepts, Greek and Hebrew word studies, prophecy, and Dead Sea Scroll archaeology. The 36 curation decisions are tied to exact root passage anchors on both endpoints and preserve each authored edge’s direction. The curation describes the actual passage claim and keeps later theological or literary interpretation distinct from the source text.

Textual examples include Paul’s uses of “with Christ” in Galatians 2:19–21, Colossians 3:1–4, and Ephesians 2:4–6; Adam as τύπος in Romans 5:12–19; the παρουσία and resurrection language in 1 Thessalonians 4 and 1 Corinthians 15; the Abba/adoption link in Romans 8:14–17; sanctification in 1 Thessalonians 4:1–8; and Paul’s explicit use of τύποι for the wilderness generation in 1 Corinthians 10. The curation marks “union with Christ” and “Trinity” as interpretive labels and does not project later technical formulations back into a single verse.

Greek and Hebrew word links use narrow textual settings. Psalm 2:2 places Hebrew מְשִׁיחוֹ alongside the Septuagint’s χριστοῦ; this is recorded as a translation correspondence in that psalm, not as complete lexical equivalence across both languages. In Genesis 9, the Septuagint uses διαθήκη for בְּרִית; the NETS material describes this as a recurring translation correspondence while noting the broader semantic range of the Greek term. [Septuagint Psalm 2 at BiblIndex](https://mxc.biblindex.org/en/bible/septuaginta/ps-2), [NETS Genesis](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/01-gen-nets.pdf).

First Peter 1:16 explicitly introduces its holiness saying as written and receives Leviticus 19:2. The curation records the reception while noting differences among the Hebrew, Septuagint, and New Testament wording. [Tyndale Bulletin, “The Use of the Old Testament for Christian Ethics in 1 Peter”](https://tyndalebulletin.org/api/v1/articles/31657-the-use-of-the-old-testament-for-christian-ethics-in-1-peter.pdf). John 14:26 identifies the promised παράκλητος as the Holy Spirit; the entry distinguishes that textual identification from treating παράκλητος and πνεῦμα as synonyms. First John 1:5–10 grounds the light/darkness pair, while Micah 6:6–8 situates מִשְׁפָּט alongside covenant loyalty and humble walking.

The Qumran cave/Dead Sea Scrolls pair is limited to the manuscript evidence for Isaiah 40:1–11. The Israel Museum’s Digital Dead Sea Scrolls project identifies the Great Isaiah Scroll among the original Qumran discoveries. This does not imply that every Dead Sea Scroll came from Qumran or that one community authored the whole collection. [Israel Museum, Digital Dead Sea Scrolls](https://dss.collections.imj.org.il/).

New Jerusalem material in Revelation 21 is described as apocalyptic canonical reception without asserting a verbatim quotation or a literal city plan. The Ezekiel 36 connection is explicitly thematic: that passage speaks of restoration, a new heart, and a new spirit but does not use *berit*. Isaiah 11’s Davidic ruler is connected with later messianic interpretation while preserving its original monarchic horizon as an open question.

## Held and excluded decisions

- **Held (29):** includes the Gezer and Hazor book-level edges. Although 1 Kings 9:15–17 names the sites, the exact 1 Kings endpoint’s root scope is 9:1–9; no chapter-level scope was inherited. The dating and identification of excavated Iron Age structures remain debated. The Qumran settlement-to-book and site-to-cave edges were held because the specific manuscript provenance belongs to scroll/cave objects, not the broader site edge. Davidic and Christological links were held where the generic edge did not identify a specific reception text. The divine-name word-study matrix was held because a common proof text does not make YHWH, Elohim, Adonai, Kyrios, and Theos interchangeable. [Keimer, “The Solomonic Gate at Gezer in Its Context”](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jjar/files/keimer_2021_jjar_1_68-101.pdf), [Oxford Academic, “The Archaeology of Memory: Solomon, History, and Biblical Representation”](https://doi.org/10.1093/0195177967.003.0005).
- **Excluded (360):** generic blank-note `related-concept` edges, broad word-study pairings, and concept tags without a distinct passage-specific claim. Exclusion applies to the authored relationship only; canonical CKL source objects remain unchanged.
