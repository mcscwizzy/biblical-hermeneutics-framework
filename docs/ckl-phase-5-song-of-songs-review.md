## Corrections made

- Removed the inherited generic wisdom placeholder, including Job, David,
  suffering, praise, lament, temple, generic court, generic audience, and
  unqualified Solomonic material.
- Rebuilt the record around the superscription; lyric sequences; awakening
  refrains; searches; body descriptions; garden, vineyard, banquet, city, and
  procession scenes; and the concluding sequence in 8:5–14.
- Distinguished the woman, man, daughters of Jerusalem, brothers, companions,
  watchmen, Solomon, and uncertain speakers without importing a complete
  dramatic cast into the Hebrew text.
- Distinguished Hebrew love lyric, lyric sequence, dialogue, *wasf* or
  body-description poem, search poem, invitation, refrain, garden and
  vineyard poem, procession poem, and wisdom-like saying.
- Qualified the superlative title, Solomonic association, authorship,
  collection and unity, female-authorship proposals, linguistic dating, and
  possible monarchic, Persian, or Hellenistic layers.
- Preserved uncertainty concerning plot, dream scenes, the lovers'
  relationship status, geography, age, ethnicity, class, and the Shulammite's
  identity.
- Addressed embodied erotic love, reciprocal speech and initiative, desire,
  praise, absence, search, body, gaze, gardens, vineyards, labor, animals,
  plants, spices, fragrance, royalty, wealth, family, jealousy, death, flame,
  water, and love's resistance to purchase.
- Distinguished the poetry's erotic force from pornography, ownership,
  entitlement, coercion, compulsory marriage, prosperity teaching, and one
  mandatory gender or relationship script.
- Treated the awakening refrains as context-sensitive poetry rather than a
  universal purity-culture timetable.
- Named the watchmen's assault in 5:7 without romanticizing it or blaming the
  woman for searching in the city.
- Treated “black or dark and beautiful” in 1:5 as mutually affirming and
  documented the need to resist colorist, racialized, and classist reception.
- Qualified *shalhebetyah* as possibly “flame of Yah” or an intensive “mighty
  flame,” rather than claiming that its morphology is settled.
- Addressed Masoretic Song, 4Q106, 4Q107, 4Q108, 6Q6, Old Greek *Asma
  asmaton*, and the Greek translation's consistently literal profile.
- Used Egyptian and Mesopotamian love poetry as genre and imagery comparison
  without asserting direct borrowing, fertility cult, sacred marriage, or
  funerary ritual.
- Distinguished the poems' human erotic sense from later Jewish God-Israel,
  Christian Christ-church, and Word-soul receptions, while documenting those
  traditions as theologically and historically important.
- Added safeguards concerning consent, marital rape, sexual and domestic
  abuse, stalking, institutional violence, purity culture, body shame,
  colorism, racism, gender stereotypes, singleness, asexuality, infertility,
  disability, aging, trauma, widowhood, divorce, and celibate vocation.
- Added fourteen sourced claims, twenty-one current-taxonomy interpretive
  notes, eighteen source records, fourteen URL-bearing external sources, nine
  graph relationships, explicit section statuses and knowledge layers, a
  populated hermeneutical lens, and retrieval metadata.

## Human review checklist

Verify:

- every grammatical speaker assignment and every intentionally uncertain
  line, including modern translation headings;
- the bounds and function of each refrain, search, invitation, body
  description, garden scene, procession, vineyard saying, and final summons;
- whether the Song is anthology, unified poem, lyric sequence, drama,
  wedding cycle, dream sequence, wisdom instruction, cultic text, or a
  combination;
- the superscription's syntax and every conclusion drawn from Solomon's name;
- all authorship, collection, editing, female-authorship, unity, audience,
  performance, and institutional-setting proposals;
- linguistic dating, loanwords, dialectal features, archaisms, alleged Greek
  influence, and the termini supplied by the Qumran manuscripts;
- whether monarchic, Persian, or Hellenistic contexts are stated at the right
  confidence level;
- the relationship of Jerusalem, Tirzah, Lebanon, En-gedi, Gilead, Carmel,
  Baal-hamon, rural settings, and royal imagery to real and imagined space;
- social class, agricultural labor, vineyard control, brothers, household
  oversight, merchants, royal wealth, and the woman's self-assertion;
- relationship status, wedding language, age, ethnicity, and the identity of
  the Shulammite without filling narrative gaps;
- *shir hashirim*, the Solomonic lamed, *dod*, *ra'yah*, *ahavah*, *nefesh*,
  *shehorah we-na'wah*, flower and fruit terms, *gan na'ul*, *kerem*,
  *Shulammith*, *qana*, *reshef*, *shalhebetyah*, *mawet*, *sheol*, and
  *mehir* in every cited context;
- the syntax and ethics of 1:5–6 without importing ancient or modern skin,
  race, beauty, labor, and class categories into one another;
- the awakening refrains' speaker, oath formula, animals, syntax, “until,”
  personification of love, and relation to surrounding scenes;
- the watchmen in 3:1–4 and 5:2–8, including violence, stripping, authority,
  dream proposals, social vulnerability, and trauma-aware reception;
- all body-description conventions, gaze, mutuality, objectification,
  botanical and zoological identifications, body diversity, and disability;
- consent, invitation, opening and delay, absence, pursuit, family power,
  jealousy, exclusivity, possession, and mutual belonging;
- love's comparison to death and Sheol, floods, the possible divine flame,
  and rejected purchase in 8:6–7;
- the contents, dates, hands, orthography, variants, and limits of 4Q106,
  4Q107, 4Q108, and 6Q6;
- the Old Greek base text, date, title, translation profile, variants, and
  modern editorial speaker labels;
- Egyptian and Mesopotamian comparisons without assuming dependence,
  fertility ritual, sacred marriage, or a funerary setting beyond evidence;
- Genesis, Psalm 45, Proverbs, prophetic marriage imagery, and every New
  Testament bride connection at the right level of quotation, allusion,
  analogy, typology, and reception;
- Jewish canon and Passover reception without projecting later practice into
  composition;
- Jewish allegorical, Christian christological, ecclesial, mystical,
  monastic, Marian, feminist, queer, postcolonial, and literal-erotic readings
  with their tradition-specific claims clearly identified;
- all safeguards concerning consent, abuse, stalking, victim-blaming, purity
  culture, race, colorism, body shame, gender roles, singleness, asexuality,
  infertility, disability, aging, trauma, divorce, widowhood, and celibacy;
- every source locator, support target, certainty label, dispute label, and
  pastoral application.

Do not advance the record merely because automated checks pass. Advance only
the sections actually reviewed, and record reviewer identity and date.
