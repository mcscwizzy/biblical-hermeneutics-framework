### Published Commentary

### Reading the Conversation Carefully

Jesus’ statement that “salvation is from the Jews” places his own mission and the story of salvation within the Jewish people and Scripture. It should not be read as erasing Jewish identity or treating Samaritans as inferior. Likewise, the Gospel’s references to “Jews” have context-sensitive meanings; they should not be turned into a description of a single hostile ethnic group. The Samaritan woman is presented as a sustained conversation partner and then as a witness whose testimony leads others to Jesus. Although Jesus names her marital history, the passage does not say that she was adulterous or a prostitute, so readers should not add that label to her story.

_Passages: John 4:19-26, John 4:4-42, John 4:9-22_

### Reading the conversation carefully

Several cautions help with this conversation. Jesus’ statement that “salvation is from the Jews” places him and salvation’s story within the Jewish people and their Scriptures; it should not be read as erasing Jewish identity or treating Samaritans as inferior. The Gospel’s term often translated “the Jews” can refer differently according to context—to Jewish people, Judeans, festival crowds, or particular authorities—so it should not automatically be treated as a single hostile ethnic group. The woman is also an extended theological conversation partner and later a witness to her city. Although Jesus speaks of her previous husbands, the passage does not say that she was guilty of adultery or prostitution, so those labels go beyond what the text states.
