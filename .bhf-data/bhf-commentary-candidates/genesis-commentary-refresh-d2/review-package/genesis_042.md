## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

Famine drives Jacob’s sons from Canaan to Egypt for grain. Joseph recognizes them, but they do not recognize him; the account does not explain why. His accusation of spying and demand that they bring Benjamin function as his testing strategy, rather than being presented as a standard legal procedure. After three days, he sends most of them home with grain while keeping Simeon until they return.

### Things Easy to Miss

Several details connect the present crisis with the family’s earlier story. Jacob keeps Benjamin behind because he fears harm for him after Joseph’s loss. When the brothers bow before Joseph, he remembers his dreams. Later, the brothers recall Joseph’s past distress and Reuben’s objection, while Joseph—though speaking through an interpreter—understands them and turns aside to weep.

### Within Genesis

Genesis moves from the universal beginnings of chapters 1–11 into the ancestral family narratives of chapters 12–50. Genesis 42 belongs to that family-focused portion of the book, which concludes with Jacob’s household in Egypt.
