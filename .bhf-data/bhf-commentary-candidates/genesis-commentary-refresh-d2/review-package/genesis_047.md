## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

Joseph formally presents members of his family to Pharaoh as livestock keepers and secures their residence in Goshen. The chapter later returns to that settlement: while Egyptians accept service to Pharaoh after receiving food, Israel gains property in Goshen and increases greatly. These scenes place the family’s secure settlement alongside the wider crisis affecting the land.

*Verses:* Genesis 47:1-6, Genesis 47:25-27<br>

### Famine

The famine is not a backdrop to the chapter but its driving condition: food has failed so severely that both Egypt and Canaan are described as languishing. This explains both the family’s request for pasture in Egypt and the Egyptians’ increasingly urgent exchanges for grain.

*Verses:* Genesis 47:13<br>
*Evidence IDs:* g6b-47-famine-scarcity<br>

### Food, Land, and Service

The account traces a sequence of exchanges during the famine. Grain is first bought with money; after money is exhausted, livestock is exchanged for food; then the people offer their land and themselves to Pharaoh for food and seed. Joseph relocates the population to cities, provides seed for sowing, and establishes Pharaoh’s continuing one-fifth share of the harvest, while four parts remain for seed and household food. Priests are treated differently because they receive an allowance from Pharaoh and do not sell their land.

Two personal scenes frame the public crisis. Jacob blesses Pharaoh, describes his 130 years as few and difficult in comparison with his ancestors, and later requires Joseph’s sworn promise not to bury him in Egypt but in the ancestral burial place. The hand-under-thigh gesture is joined in the passage to that solemn oath. The family’s allotted land is also called the land of Rameses; the chapter uses this as the place-name for their settlement.

### Genesis in View

Genesis moves from its opening account of universal origins to the ancestral family narratives that occupy its later chapters. Genesis 47 therefore belongs near the end of that larger movement, as Jacob’s household becomes settled in Egypt.
