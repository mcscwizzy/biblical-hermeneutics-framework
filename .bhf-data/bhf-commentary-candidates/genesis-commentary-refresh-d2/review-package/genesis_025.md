## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

The chapter closes Abraham’s story while establishing Isaac as the heir. Abraham gives his estate to Isaac, but gives gifts to the sons of his concubines and sends them eastward; the passage distinguishes their provision from Isaac’s inheritance. Isaac and Ishmael then bury Abraham at Machpelah, where Sarah was buried. The account next turns to Isaac’s household: Rebekah’s barrenness ends after Isaac entreats God, and the birth of the twins begins the next generation’s story.

### Family Lines and the Oracle

The genealogies of Keturah’s descendants and Ishmael’s sons preserve Abraham’s wider family alongside Isaac’s line. Ishmael’s twelve sons are called princes, and their settlements are described from Havilah toward Shur; later attempts to identify every named descendant with a later tribe remain uncertain. Rebekah’s twins are also described in collective terms: the oracle speaks of two nations and two peoples, with the older serving the younger. Within Genesis, this explains why the brothers’ birth is presented as more than an ordinary family event.

*Verses:* Genesis 25:1-4, Genesis 25:12-18, Genesis 25:22-23<br>

### The Brothers and the Birthright

Esau’s birthright is more than an extra share of property; it concerns his broader standing within the kinship group and household. The oath makes the sale a formal transfer, and the narrator’s final statement that Esau “despised” his birthright gives the episode its evaluative force. The birth scene also links Jacob’s name with his grasp of Esau’s heel, although the precise etymology and what this implies about Jacob’s character remain debated.

*Verses:* Genesis 25:19-21, Genesis 25:24-26, Genesis 25:27-34, Genesis 25:5-6, Genesis 25:7-11<br>

### Genesis in View

Genesis moves from the universal beginnings of chapters 1–11 into the ancestral family narratives of chapters 12–50, ending with Jacob’s household in Egypt. Genesis 25 stands at a hinge within that family story: Abraham’s account is brought to a close, and the narrative turns decisively toward Isaac and then Jacob.
