## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

Genesis 46 tells how Jacob’s whole household moves from Canaan into Egypt. Before the journey, God addresses Jacob in a night vision, telling him not to fear the move, promising to be with him there and to make his family into a great nation. The account then stresses the scale of the relocation: sons, grandchildren, wives, livestock, possessions, and Pharaoh’s wagons all travel together.

*Verses:* Genesis 46:16-18, Genesis 46:2-4, Genesis 46:28, Genesis 46:5-7<br>
*Evidence IDs:* g6b-46-judah-sent-ahead, g6b-46-night-vision-promise, g6b-46-serah-and-zilpah, g6b-46-wagons-and-descent<br>

The chapter’s long family list is not simply a pause in the story. It identifies the household by maternal family lines—Leah, Zilpah, Rachel, and Bilhah—and includes descendants such as Joseph’s sons, who had been born in Egypt. The list frames the migration as the movement of an extended household rather than only Jacob and his immediate sons.

### Beer-sheba

At Beer-sheba, Jacob offers sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac before leaving for Egypt. This stops the journey at a moment of worship before the family crosses into the next stage of the narrative.

### Joseph and Jacob

The arrival in Goshen leads to the chapter’s emotional center: Joseph comes to meet Jacob, embraces him, and weeps. Jacob’s words that he can now die after seeing Joseph alive show that this reunion resolves the fear that Joseph had been lost to him.

### Counting the household and settling in Goshen

The chapter distinguishes sixty-six family members who came with Jacob from a total of seventy when Joseph and his two Egypt-born sons are included. Some ancient textual traditions instead give a total of seventy-five, including the Greek form of Genesis; fragments from Qumran and Acts 7:14 also reflect that number. The differing totals arise from different ways of presenting the household list, so readers may encounter either number in translations and related passages.

*Verses:* Genesis 46:19-25, Genesis 46:26-27, Genesis 46:27, Genesis 46:4, Genesis 46:8-15<br>
*Evidence IDs:* g6b-46-bilhah-and-rachel, g6b-46-bring-up-again, g6b-46-genealogical-list-leah, g6b-46-lxx-seventy-five, g6b-46-mt-counts-66-70<br>
*Synthesis IDs:* syn_interpretive_questions_af560e4d3ac6

Joseph prepares his family for Pharaoh’s questions by having them identify themselves as keepers of livestock. The chapter says that Egyptians regard shepherds as an abomination and presents this as the reason the family may live in Goshen. Their occupation is therefore part of how the settlement is negotiated within the story.

### Genesis in its larger literary setting

Genesis moves from its account of universal origins in chapters 1–11 to the ancestral family narratives in chapters 12–50. Genesis 46 belongs near the end of that family story, as Jacob’s household arrives in Egypt. Questions about whether Genesis should be attributed to Moses or understood through compositional models remain distinct interpretive positions; Genesis itself does not narrate its own literary formation.
