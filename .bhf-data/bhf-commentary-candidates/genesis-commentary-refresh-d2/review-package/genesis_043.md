## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

The famine makes a second journey unavoidable, but Benjamin must accompany his brothers. Judah accepts personal responsibility for Benjamin’s safe return, while Jacob sends a present and both new payment and the silver previously found in their sacks. The returned silver is treated as a transaction needing correction; the chapter does not describe a complete Egyptian accounting procedure.

### At Joseph’s House

Being brought into Joseph’s house alarms the brothers because they fear the returned silver will be used against them. Instead, the steward reassures them, provides hospitality, and brings Simeon out. At the meal, the brothers are seated in birth order and are astonished; the narrative records their surprise but does not explain it by saying that Joseph used divination.

*Verses:* Genesis 43:16-25, Genesis 43:33<br>
*Evidence IDs:* g6a-43-birth-order-seating, g6a-43-steward-hospitality<br>

### Joseph, Benjamin, and Simeon

Simeon’s release resolves the condition left from the earlier journey. Joseph then asks after his father and sees Benjamin, identified by the narrator as his brother through the same mother. His withdrawal to weep is presented as an immediate response within the scene, without requiring a further diagnosis of his inner state.

*Verses:* Genesis 43:23, Genesis 43:26-30<br>
*Evidence IDs:* g6a-43-joseph-benjamin, g6a-43-simeon-release<br>

### Details at the Meal

The Egyptians eat separately from the Hebrews, and Joseph is also served separately. The text calls shared eating with Hebrews an abomination to the Egyptians, but the reason for that boundary is debated; it should not simply be equated with a modern racial category. Benjamin’s portion is five times larger than the others’, though explanations that treat this as a fixed honor custom remain proposals rather than a universal rule.

*Verses:* Genesis 43:31-32, Genesis 43:34<br>

Genesis moves from the universal beginnings in chapters 1–11 into the ancestral family narratives of chapters 12–50, ending with Jacob’s household in Egypt. This chapter belongs to that family narrative movement.
