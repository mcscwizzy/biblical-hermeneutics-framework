## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

Genesis 21 moves through three connected scenes: Isaac’s birth, Hagar and Ishmael’s departure, and Abraham’s agreement with Abimelech. Isaac’s birth is presented as occurring at the time God had spoken; his name is linked with Sarah’s laughter and joy, and his circumcision follows God’s command. The family conflict is not treated as routine household management: Abraham is distressed, Isaac is identified as the line through which Abraham’s offspring will be called, and Ishmael is nevertheless promised a future as a great nation. In the wilderness, Hagar expects the boy to die, but God hears him and shows her water. The chapter closes with a disputed well resolved through an oath and seven ewe lambs set apart as testimony at Beer-sheba.

### Ishmael’s Future

Ishmael is not left in the story simply as Isaac’s rival. God promises that he too will become a great nation because he is Abraham’s offspring, and the narrative says that God is with him as he grows. This keeps the chapter’s distinction between Isaac and Ishmael from becoming a warrant for ethnic stereotyping.

### Wilderness and Settlement

The account names two wilderness settings without supplying precise modern boundaries. Hagar and Ishmael are sent into the wilderness of Beer-sheba, while Ishmael later lives in the wilderness of Paran; his mother obtains a wife for him from Egypt. The chapter also returns to Beer-sheba as the place where Abraham and Abimelech settle the well dispute. Abraham’s planting of a tamarisk and invocation of the everlasting God close that settlement scene, but the passage does not provide enough detail to reconstruct the tree, a shrine, or the well archaeologically.

### Genesis in Its Wider Story

Genesis 21 belongs to the book’s ancestral-family narratives. Genesis first tells of universal beginnings in chapters 1–11, then follows the families of the ancestors in chapters 12–50, ending with Jacob’s household in Egypt. This chapter’s attention to Abraham’s sons, household conflict, and local agreements belongs within that later family-centered movement.
