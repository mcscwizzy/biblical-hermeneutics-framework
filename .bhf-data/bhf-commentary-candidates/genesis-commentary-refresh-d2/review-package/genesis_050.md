## Candidate Commentary

### Overview

Genesis closes Jacob’s burial with an unresolved family fear. Joseph obtains Pharaoh’s permission to fulfill his oath to bury Jacob at Machpelah in Canaan, but after Jacob’s death his brothers fear that Joseph may now repay their earlier wrong. Joseph weeps, rejects their fear, and promises to provide for them and their children. His statement that they intended evil while God intended good identifies the preservation of many lives as the outcome he recognizes in their shared story.

### Mourning and burial

The account lingers over Jacob’s death rites: physicians embalm him for forty days, Egypt mourns for seventy days, and a large company including Pharaoh’s officials, chariots, and horsemen accompanies the family toward Canaan. The procession mourns again at the threshing floor of Atad, a place the narrative explains by giving it the name Abel-mizraim. Jacob’s burial is therefore narrated as a public journey from Egypt to the family burial place in Canaan.

### A message from the brothers

The brothers report that Jacob commanded Joseph to forgive them, but Genesis has not previously narrated Jacob giving that instruction. The passage does not settle whether this was an unrecorded command or a message framed by brothers who remained afraid. What it does show clearly is their continuing anxiety after Jacob’s death and Joseph’s compassionate response.

*Verses:* Genesis 50:16-17, Genesis 50:24-26<br>
*Evidence IDs:* g6b-50-oath-bones-coffin, g6b-50-reported-message<br>

### An ending in Egypt

The book ends with Joseph’s death and burial in Egypt, not with the family established in the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Joseph’s request that his bones be carried up when God brings the family from Egypt keeps that expectation open at the book’s conclusion.

*Verses:* Genesis 50:24-26<br>
*Evidence IDs:* genesis-ends-in-egypt<br>

### Genesis in view

Genesis as a whole moves from accounts of universal origins in chapters 1–11 to the narratives of the ancestral family in chapters 12–50. Its final scene leaves Jacob’s household in Egypt, so Genesis 50 functions both as the end of Joseph’s story and as the unfinished ending of the family’s location.
