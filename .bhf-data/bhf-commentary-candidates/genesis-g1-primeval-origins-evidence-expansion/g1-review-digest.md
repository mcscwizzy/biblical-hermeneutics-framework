### Genesis 2

Evidence now covers the 2:4 *toledot* hinge and the debated relation between Genesis 1 and 2; dust/ground wordplay and divine breath; garden planting and presence; the four rivers and limits on locating Eden; working/keeping the garden; the two trees and disputed “knowing good and evil” language; *ezer kenegdo* as a corresponding partner; kinship and one-flesh language; and nakedness without shame. The sequence and observations are grounded in Genesis, with interpretive questions bounded by Sarna, Hamilton, Schmid, Amit, and Kelly. Schmid’s account of the relationship between the creation units is retained as a scholarly position rather than a verdict. [Schmid](https://www.thetorah.com/article/genesis-two-creation-accounts-compiled-and-interpreted-as-one), [Amit](https://thetorah.com/article/the-exposition-of-the-garden-of-eden-story), [Kelly](https://thetorah.com/article/the-secret-of-the-garden-of-eden-knowledge-or-immortality).

The river names and regional descriptions are recorded without coordinates. Pishon and Gihon remain unidentified, and the candidate does not treat a proposed Eden location as established. The immediate text describes garden work; possible priestly verbal parallels remain an interpretation. The phrase *ezer kenegdo* is not used to establish a social hierarchy or a technical equality formula.

### Genesis 3

Evidence distinguishes the serpent’s description in Genesis from later canonical identification in Revelation; treats the tree, knowing, deception, and human choice without reducing the tree to generic knowledge; traces nakedness, sewing, hiding, and divine clothing; and records the question sequence and movement of blame. The serpent, woman, man, and ground receive separate judgment/oracle evidence. Adam/*adamah* wordplay remains in view.

Genesis 3:15 is represented first as offspring conflict in its immediate wording. Later Jewish and Christian interpretation, including messianic reception, is separately sourced and marked as reception rather than the sole original-context meaning. [Zhakevich](https://jbtsonline.org/2022/06/genesis-315-in-the-new-testament-and-in-the-pentateuchal-targums-enmity-as-a-spiritual-conflict-by-iosif-j-zhakevich/).

The expulsion is eastward, return is blocked, and the flaming weapon is recorded without speculative reconstruction. Cherubim are contextualized from biblical sanctuary imagery; Genesis itself gives no physical description. [Lexham Bible Dictionary, “Cherubim”](https://biblia.com/books/lbd/offset/4581289).

### Genesis 4

The offering evidence records that the text describes YHWH’s regard for Abel and his offering but does not state why Cain’s offering is not regarded. The source lock rejects the claim that Cain is rejected solely because his offering is non-blood. [Zucker](https://thetorah.com/article/why-did-cain-kill-abel).

The evidence tracks sin crouching at the door, blood crying from the ground, Cain’s agricultural loss and wandering, and the protective function of the sign without describing its form. It rejects racial identification of the sign and marks racialized readings as later harmful reception. [Junior](https://doi.org/10.15699/jbl.1394.2020.2).

Cain’s city and genealogy are recorded as narrative claims, not archaeological identifications. Jabal, Jubal, and Tubal-cain are tied to the textual craft traditions of livestock, music, and metalworking without claiming that archaeology identifies those individuals. Lamech’s two wives and violence poem are kept distinct from Cain’s protective sevenfold declaration; the Seth transition and calling on YHWH close the chapter evidence.
